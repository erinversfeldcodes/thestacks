# Verbatim Regurgitation Control in Production LLM/RAG Systems

Research notes, 2026-11-20. Scope: detection and suppression of verbatim/near-verbatim reproduction of retrieved or memorized text while preserving grounded citation — feeding an implementation gate for a Postgres+pgvector RAG system over one author's private writing corpus.

## What overlap-detection techniques are used in practice, and what thresholds distinguish quotation from paraphrase?

### Takeaway
The field has converged on a layered stack: exact-substring matching via suffix arrays or n-gram Bloom filters at a canonical ~50-token threshold for "memorized/verbatim," LCS- and ROUGE-based near-duplicate metrics for lightly edited copies, MinHash/Jaccard (~0.8) for near-duplicate passages, and embedding cosine similarity for semantic-only overlap. No single lexical threshold cleanly separates quotation from paraphrase; production evaluations (CoTaEval) therefore score a battery of seven metrics rather than one.

### Cited Findings
- **50 verbatim tokens is the canonical "memorized" threshold.** Nasr/Carlini et al. count output as memorized training data if it contains "a substring of length at least 50 tokens that is contained verbatim in the training set," checked by binary search over a suffix array built on the corpus (their 9TB AuxDataset produced a ~45TB suffix array, sharded 32 ways). The value 50 was chosen empirically by doubling an estimate of chance news-article overlap so that coincidental matches are unlikely; re-running at 40 and 100 tokens preserved the same model rankings. They additionally require the generated text to have high entropy, filtering out degenerate output (repeated whitespace, number lists) — [Scalable Extraction of Training Data from (Production) Language Models, arXiv:2311.17035](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2311.17035)
- **Approximate-verbatim criterion:** the same paper uses a looser automated proxy — flag an output if ≥80% of its tokens fall inside 50-grams present in the reference corpus. Manual web search found ~2x more true memorization than the corpus-match criterion (150 of 494 sampled outputs vs 70), i.e., exact corpus matching is a lower bound — [arXiv:2311.17035](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2311.17035)
- **The same 50-token exact-substring threshold is the de-facto standard in training-data deduplication** (Lee et al. 2021, "Deduplicating Training Data Makes Language Models Better"): suffix array over the dataset, strip repeated substrings of ≥50 tokens; adopted by RefinedWeb and MADLAD-400; GneissWeb's variant keeps the first occurrence and removes subsequent matches over 50 consecutive tokens — [arXiv:2107.06499](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.06499); [Mosaic Memory, arXiv:2405.15523](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.15523); [GneissWeb, arXiv:2502.14907](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.14907)
- **MinHash near-duplicate threshold:** DataComp-LM uses MinHash over 5-token n-grams targeting Jaccard similarity 0.8; approximate matching is standardly run as a second step after exact-substring matching because it is more expensive — [DataComp-LM, arXiv:2406.11794](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.11794). (A survey also reports a 13-gram / 450-hash-function / 0.8 configuration, but without clear attribution — treat as unverified — [arXiv:2402.16827](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.16827).)
- **CoTaEval's regurgitation metric battery** (the most implementation-ready published set): (a) character-level LCS on lowercased text with whitespace and punctuation removed; (b) word-level LCS on lowercased, punctuation-stripped, whitespace-tokenized text; (c) ROUGE-1 and ROUGE-L reported as recall so output length doesn't distort scores; (d) word-level ACS = cumulative word count of all matching subsequences longer than 3 words (catches "stitched" quoting that LCS misses); (e) Levenshtein distance; (f) MinHash Jaccard similarity over 3-gram sets; (g) semantic cosine similarity of all-MiniLM-L6-v2 embeddings. The paper reports win rates rather than fixed pass/fail thresholds and notes the metrics "may not be dispositive of infringement" — [CoTaEval, arXiv:2406.18664v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.18664v4)
- **MemFree decoding** (Ippolito et al.): a decoding-time guard that checks each candidate token against training-data n-grams stored in a Bloom filter and rejects any token that would complete a training n-gram — guarantees zero verbatim n-gram reproduction and scales to hundreds of GB of text — [Preventing Verbatim Memorization in Language Models Gives a False Sense of Privacy, arXiv:2210.17546](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.17546). CoTaEval's replication tested n ∈ {6, 12, 24} and reports n=6 — [arXiv:2406.18664v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.18664v4)
- **BloomScrub (EMNLP 2025)** operationalizes detect-then-rewrite: a Bloom filter scans output n-grams against the protected corpus to flag matching spans (including near-verbatim stitched quotes); flagged quotes are fed to a rewrite model; detection and rewriting alternate; if long quotes cannot be removed the system abstains, yielding a formal ("certified") guarantee of no long verbatim quotes — [Certified Mitigation of Worst-Case LLM Copyright Infringement, arXiv:2504.16046](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.16046v2); [ACL Anthology 2025.emnlp-main.1784](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1784/)
- **Lexical-vs-paraphrase gap is measurable:** CopyBench distinguishes literal copying (verbatim overlap) from non-literal copying (same content, different surface form — "event copying" and "character copying"); literal copying rose from 0.2% to 10.5% and non-literal from 2.3% to 5.9% going from Llama3-8B to 70B — [CopyBench, arXiv:2407.07087](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.07087v1)
- **Plagiarism-detection literature offers no canonical token cutoff:** longest-common-substring (Gusfield 1997 lineage) works well on verbatim copy-paste via suffix trees but weakens under rewording; thresholds "are typically chosen empirically"; paraphrase mechanisms tend to shorten copied text and more complex paraphrase defeats lexical detectors — [COLING 2012 study](https://download.hrz.tu-darmstadt.de/pub/FB20/Dekanat/Publikationen/UKP/COLING_2012_DaB_published.pdf); [Wikipedia: Content similarity detection](https://Www.Wikipedia.org/wiki/Plagiarism_checker)
- **Membership-scoring aid:** Nasr et al. used a likelihood-ratio score (model perplexity / zlib-compressed entropy) to separate memorized from non-memorized output with high precision — [arXiv:2311.17035](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2311.17035)

### Inferences
- A practical gate for a single-author private corpus (small enough for an in-Postgres index) can implement the full research stack cheaply: (1) exact layer — a table of hashed word n-grams (n≈6–8, normalized: lowercase, punctuation/whitespace stripped) over the corpus; flag any output window where consecutive matched n-grams accumulate; (2) near-dup layer — word-level LCS and ACS (>3-word subsequences) against the retrieved chunks specifically, since in RAG the likely source is in-context; (3) semantic layer — pgvector cosine against corpus chunk embeddings to catch paraphrase-level reproduction, scored but not necessarily blocked.
- Threshold calibration transfer: ~50 tokens ≈ unambiguous verbatim reproduction (block); CoTaEval's normalization recipe (lowercase, strip whitespace/punct before LCS) is the right canonicalization because models evade exact matchers with trivial edits (see Q3); matches of ≤3 words are noise by CoTaEval's own ACS floor. The actionable gray band is roughly 8–50 normalized tokens, where distinctiveness (entropy/IDF) should decide, per the entropy filter in Nasr et al. and Copilot's "too generic" failure class (Q2).
- Suffix arrays/automata are the exhaustive tool (offline audit); Bloom filters over n-grams are the online tool (per-response latency). For a corpus of one author's writing, both fit in memory; a Postgres GIN index over n-gram hashes is an adequate substitute for either.

### Gaps
- No published source gives a validated numeric threshold that separates "quotation" from "paraphrase" in tokens — every system picks empirically (CoTaEval reports distributions, not cutoffs; plagiarism literature says thresholds are empirical).
- The false-positive rate of MemFree's Bloom filter is not reported in the retrieved material.

## How do deployed systems implement "cite but don't recite," and what are their failure modes?

### Takeaway
Four deployed regimes exist: GitHub Copilot's duplication filter (block suggestions whose ~150-character normalized lexeme window matches indexed public code, or surface the match + license via "code referencing" instead of blocking), Azure's optional protected-material filters run on completions, Google Gemini's non-configurable RECITATION finish-reason that kills the response, and Anthropic's court-stipulated lyrics output filters. Documented failure modes: generic/short snippets pass, restructured text passes, false positives on the user's own supplied content (Gemini), silent empty responses that break parsers, and adversarial "plead until it recites" prompting (the Perplexity/News Corp litigation).

### Cited Findings
- **GitHub Copilot duplication filter:** when enabled, Copilot checks each suggestion together with ~150 characters of surrounding code against an index of all public code on GitHub.com; it strips all whitespace and compares lexemes of the suggestion against lexemes indexed from public repositories; on a match the suggestion is discarded and the next in the queue is tried — [GitHub Docs: finding public code that matches suggestions](https://docs.github.com/en/copilot/how-tos/get-code-suggestions/find-matching-code?tool=webui); [Microsoft Learn training module](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/training/modules/github-copilot-management-and-customizations/3-github-copilot-contractual-protections-disabling-matching-public-code)
- **Copilot failure modes:** the filter does not catch short snippets and common patterns too generic to flag, nor code restructured or partially modified from its source — [DataCamp Copilot privacy guide](https://www.datacamp.com/tutorial/github-copilot-privacy-and-troubleshooting)
- **Copilot "code referencing" is the cite-don't-block alternative:** instead of suppressing, it shows the matching public code, its repository, and its license so the developer can attribute or avoid — [GitHub blog: Introducing code referencing](https://github.blog/engineering/introducing-code-referencing-for-github-copilot/); [GA announcement incl. Azure AI](https://github.blog/news-insights/product-news/code-referencing-now-generally-available-in-github-copilot-and-with-microsoft-azure-ai)
- **Azure OpenAI protected-material filters** (optional, off by default): a text filter for "known text content (for example, song lyrics, articles, recipes, and selected web content)," English-only; a code filter flagging matches to public GitHub repositories which "provides citations and licensing information" rather than only blocking; Microsoft says the filters are meant to run on LLM completions, not user prompts — [Microsoft Learn: content filter protected material](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-foundry/openai/concepts/content-filter-protected-material); [Azure AI Content Safety: protected material concept](https://learn.microsoft.com/azure/ai-services/content-safety/concepts/protected-material); [GA blog](https://techcommunity.microsoft.com/blog/aiazureaiservicesblog/ga-release-of-protected-material-detection-in-azure-ai-content/4235577)
- **Google Gemini RECITATION:** generation is stopped with finishReason=RECITATION when "the output may resemble certain data"; Google's documented remediations are to make the prompt/context more unique and raise temperature; developers treat it as retryable because resending the same prompt sometimes passes — [Gemini API troubleshooting guide](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/troubleshooting.md.txt); [forum: retryable](https://discuss.ai.google.dev/t/no-response-due-to-recitation-finishreason/3957)
- **Gemini RECITATION failure modes:** repeated false positives on the user's *own* content supplied in context — translating one's own website, processing one's own legal documents, transcribing believed-public-domain audio — and a blocked candidate can return empty content with no role field, crashing strict SDK parsers; no documented way to disable the filter — [forum: own content via API](https://discuss.ai.google.dev/t/finishreason-recitation-issue-wtih-my-own-content-via-api/69104); [forum: own legal documents + parser crash](https://discuss.ai.google.dev/t/finishreason-recitation-on-users-own-legal-documents-also-returns-content-with-no-role-crashing-strict-sdk-parsers/171706); [forum: audio](https://discuss.ai.google.dev/t/recitation-error-issue-w-audio/57825)
- **Anthropic lyrics guardrails (court-stipulated, Jan 2025):** Anthropic and eight music publishers filed an approved stipulation under which Anthropic maintains its existing output guardrails against reproducing copyrighted lyrics and applies them to future models; Anthropic may change how the guardrails work provided changes do not weaken them, and publishers get a flagging avenue if guardrails fail; the court later declined a broader training-data injunction, noting much of the potential harm was resolved by the guardrail deal — [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/us-law-week/anthropic-agrees-to-enforce-copyright-guardrails-on-new-ai-tools); [Tech Policy Press analysis](https://techpolicy.press/analysis-what-anthropics-deal-with-music-publishers-does-and-doesnt-do); [Hollywood Reporter](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/business/business-news/anthropic-enforce-copyright-guardrails-ai-tools-1236098152/)
- **News-RAG litigation as a failure-mode catalog:** Dow Jones/New York Post v. Perplexity (S.D.N.Y., Oct 2024) alleges Perplexity's answer engine "at times reproduces the content verbatim" from its RAG database of articles, without credit or links; Perplexity disputes that it regurgitates full articles, says its guardrails were "working as intended," and claims plaintiffs used "increasingly pointed queries, until they were pleading with Perplexity to repeat their articles verbatim" (an adversarial-extraction pattern); as of the retrieved reporting (through March 2026 discovery disputes) no merits ruling — [TechCrunch on the suit](https://techcrunch.com/2024/10/21/content-kleptocracy-news-corp-outlets-sue-perplexity-over-scraped-stories); [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/ip-law/dow-jones-new-york-post-sue-perplexity-ai-over-model-training); [Press Gazette on the entrapment claim](https://pressgazette.co.uk/media_law/perplexity-claims-news-corp-tried-to-entrap-chatbot-to-make-copyright-case/); [Perplexity response](https://techcrunch.com/2024/10/24/they-wish-this-technology-didnt-exist-perplexity-responds-to-news-corps-lawsuit)
- **RAG datastores leak wholesale under adversarial querying:** CopyBreakRAG extracts over 70% of a target application's knowledge-base source data verbatim via adversarial queries — [arXiv:2411.14110v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.14110v2)

### Inferences
- The two deployed postures are "block on match" (Copilot block mode, Gemini RECITATION, Anthropic lyrics filter) and "disclose on match" (Copilot code referencing, Azure code citations). For a cite-but-don't-recite product the disclose pattern is the better UX template: detect the overlap, then attribute-or-rewrite rather than returning an empty response — Gemini's silent empty-candidate behavior is the documented worst case for integrators.
- Gemini's false positives on user-owned context are directly relevant to a single-author corpus: when the corpus owner is the user, a provider-level recitation filter will fight legitimate use (the author asking to see their own text). The gate must therefore distinguish *whose* text is being reproduced and *who is asking* — provider filters cannot, which argues for application-level enforcement with an owner bypass.
- The Perplexity record shows the threat model must include cooperative-adversarial users who iteratively push for recitation; per-response filtering must hold under many retries (a stateless filter that fails 1-in-N times will be defeated by retry loops — Gemini's own "retryable" behavior demonstrates the leak).

### Gaps
- GitHub does not publish the exact lexeme-count threshold or matching algorithm beyond "~150 characters, whitespace stripped, lexeme comparison"; the precise similarity function is unconfirmed.
- Azure does not document the matching technique or thresholds behind "protected material text" detection (only its target classes), and it is English-only.
- No provider documents its filter's false-negative rate; no merits ruling yet in the Perplexity case to establish what output similarity level courts will treat as infringing in RAG.

## Prompt-side vs output-side enforcement: how well do "do not quote" instructions hold, and what layering is recommended?

### Takeaway
System-prompt instructions are the cheapest and, in RAG settings, often the single most effective intervention (CoTaEval: regurgitation win rate 25.4%→59.1% on news at ~zero utility/latency cost), but they fail unpredictably on a per-instance basis and models comply even less when copyrighted text sits in the provided context. Output-side exact filters (MemFree-style) provide hard verbatim guarantees but are trivially evaded by near-verbatim edits — which models produce spontaneously. The evidenced layering is: instruction prompt + decoding/post-hoc n-gram detector + near-duplicate (LCS/ACS) check + rewrite-or-abstain loop (BloomScrub), with periodic adversarial audit.

### Cited Findings
- **CoTaEval head-to-head (RAG setting, news, Llama2-7B-chat; "regurgitation win rate," higher = more reduction):** Vanilla 25.4%; System Prompt 59.1% with utility and speed ≈ vanilla (1.00x); Top-k logit perturbation 46.8% with large utility loss; MemFree 45.7% at 0.92x speed. Books: vanilla 23.8%, system prompt 43.5%, top-k 57.4%, MemFree 51.2%. Llama2-70B news: vanilla 15.9%, system prompt 28.4%, top-k 68.9%, MemFree 62.8% — [CoTaEval, arXiv:2406.18664v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.18664v4)
- **CoTaEval (memorization setting, fine-tuned Llama2-7B-chat, news):** Vanilla 19.6%; System Prompt 54.1%; Top-k 28.9%; MemFree 24.8%; R-CAD 41.7% at 0.53x speed; unlearning GD 64.1% / KL 61.4% / PO 66.2% but losing ~30–60% of blocklisted and in-domain utility, with ~117 GPU-hours of hyperparameter search and "no guarantee the content won't reappear" — [arXiv:2406.18664v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.18664v4)
- **Six system-prompt variants were tested; the Bing Chat prompt performed best**, but "the model does not correctly reject every instance" — high-similarity outputs still occur under the best prompt — [arXiv:2406.18664v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.18664v4)
- **MemFree's documented evasion:** exact-match detection is bypassed by minor misspellings, extra whitespace, or newline changes, and "the model tends to make these edits," which the CoTaEval authors say does not reduce risk; it also misses near-duplicates — [arXiv:2406.18664v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.18664v4)
- **Ippolito et al. (the original MemFree paper, retitled "…False Sense of Privacy"):** a perfect verbatim filter does not prevent training-data leakage; style-transfer prompts (ALL CAPS / all lowercase) bypass verbatim matching, and models "succeed at emitting approximate memorization that bypass our filter all by themselves"; conclusion: verbatim-prevention defenses are "necessary but not sufficient" — [arXiv:2210.17546](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.17546); [SoK: Memorization in LLMs](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.18362)
- **Instructions lose force when the text is in context:** models refuse direct requests for copyrighted text but comply when the same text is placed in retrieved or user-provided context, even with an explicit copyright notice present (43,200-query benchmark) — [Do LLMs Know to Respect Copyright Notice?, arXiv:2411.01136](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.01136v1)
- **CoTaEval overall conclusion:** "no tested method excels across all metrics"; system prompt + MemFree "offer some mitigation but cannot completely prevent undesirable regurgitation" — [arXiv:2406.18664 abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18664); [arXiv:2406.18664v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.18664v3)
- **BloomScrub's layering (detect → rewrite → re-check → abstain)** is the published post-hoc architecture that adds a guarantee on top of prompting: alternate Bloom-filter span detection with LLM rewriting of flagged quotes; abstain when long quotes cannot be removed — [arXiv:2504.16046](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.16046v2)
- **Alignment/RLHF is not a defense:** the divergence attack raised ChatGPT's training-data emission rate 150x over normal behavior; alignment "does not remove memorization," and standard memorization tests missed ChatGPT's exposure — [arXiv:2311.17035](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2311.17035); [ICLR 2025 version](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2025/poster/27894)
- **Training-time option (context for completeness):** CopyBench finds training-time alignment can reduce literal copying but may *increase* non-literal copying, and current inference-time mitigations "primarily reduce literal but not non-literal copying" — [arXiv:2407.07087](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.07087v1)

### Inferences
- Recommended layering for the implementation gate, directly supported by the above: (1) system-prompt rule stating the no-verbatim policy and the citation format — cheap, catches the majority of casual cases, must never be the only layer; (2) output-side detector running the normalized n-gram + LCS/ACS battery against the retrieved chunks (not the whole corpus — in RAG the in-context documents are the dominant leak source per 2411.01136); (3) on detection, a rewrite pass quoting the policy and the flagged spans back to the model (BloomScrub pattern), bounded retries; (4) hard abstain/truncate if the span survives rewriting; (5) a regression eval (CoTaEval-style win rates on an adversarial prompt set) rather than trusting any layer.
- Because the model itself drifts into near-verbatim edits under a verbatim-only filter, the gate's detector must normalize (case-fold, strip whitespace/punctuation — CoTaEval's exact recipe) and must include a near-dup metric (ACS or ROUGE-L recall) with its own threshold, or the filter will pass "the same text with different line breaks."
- Semantic-only reproduction (non-literal copying) is not reliably stoppable by any current inference-time method; for a private corpus the practical mitigation is scoring + logging semantic similarity for human review, not blocking.

### Gaps
- No published study of how instruction-following holds up across many turns of adversarial retrying in a single session (the Perplexity "pleading" pattern) — only single-shot benchmark numbers.
- CoTaEval's numbers are on Llama2-class models (plus a 70B); comparable public head-to-head numbers for 2025–26 frontier models were not found.

## How should a filter handle the gray zone: short distinctive phrases, bibliographic strings, and user-requested quotes?

### Takeaway
There is no legal or technical bright line. The research mechanisms that map onto the gray zone are: entropy/distinctiveness filters (so boilerplate and bibliographic strings pass), minimum-match floors (~3 words in CoTaEval's ACS; "too generic" snippets deliberately pass Copilot), proportionality rather than absolute length (fair-use doctrine), and an attribute-instead-of-block path for sanctioned quoting (Copilot code referencing / Azure citations). Publisher practice clusters around 200–400 words of prose as the ask-permission line, but Harper & Row shows even 300 words can be too much when it is the heart of the work.

### Cited Findings
- **Low-distinctiveness text is deliberately exempted in deployed and research systems:** Copilot's filter does not catch "short snippets and common patterns that are too generic to flag" — [DataCamp guide](https://www.datacamp.com/tutorial/github-copilot-privacy-and-troubleshooting); Nasr et al. require generated text to have high entropy before counting a match, to exclude degenerate/boilerplate output — [arXiv:2311.17035](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2311.17035); CoTaEval's ACS metric only counts matching subsequences longer than 3 words — [arXiv:2406.18664v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.18664v4)
- **No statutory word count for fair use:** "the law does not provide a specific word-count that will be designated as fair use"; in Harper & Row (Gerald Ford memoir), quoting ~300 words from a ~30,000-word manuscript was held *not* fair use because of the excerpt's centrality — [FindLaw fair-use guidelines](https://corporate.findlaw.com/intellectual-property/fair-use-interpretation-amp-guidelines-the-fair-use-doctrine.html); [OpenStax IP text](https://openstax.org/books/introduction-intellectual-property/pages/3-7-the-fair-use-defense)
- **Publisher rules of thumb vary:** ~200–300 words from a book-length work (one publisher), 400 words consecutive / 800 cumulative from a single article (an association guideline), 400 words for a single prose extract in UK fair-dealing practice — [Writer's guide to fair use and permissions](https://www.goodreads.com/author_blog_posts/18596024-a-writer-s-guide-to-fair-use-and-permissions-sample-permissions-letter?tab=book); [Leeds library fair-dealing guidance](https://library.leeds.ac.uk/info/1405/copyright/34/copyright-exceptions/2)
- **Proportion beats absolute count:** quotations should be judged proportional to the use made of them; "250 words from a 300-word poem may be less fair than 250 words from a long article"; attribution does not substitute for permission — [Duke University Press fair-use guidance](https://dukeupress.edu/journals/journal-authors-editors/permissions-information-for-journal-authors/fair-use); [UW-Madison library](https://www.library.wisc.edu/research-support/scholarly-communication/copyright-resources/using-copyrightable-materials/fair-use); [Rutgers](https://www.libraries.rutgers.edu/copyright/fair-use)
- **The attribute-don't-block precedent:** Copilot code referencing surfaces the matched source and license so the match can be kept *with attribution* instead of suppressed — [GitHub blog](https://github.blog/engineering/introducing-code-referencing-for-github-copilot/); Azure's code filter likewise returns citation and licensing information on match — [Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-foundry/openai/concepts/content-filter-protected-material)
- **Over-blocking harms legitimate owner use:** Gemini's RECITATION filter repeatedly blocks users processing their own documents, with no bypass — [Google AI forum](https://discuss.ai.google.dev/t/finishreason-recitation-issue-wtih-my-own-content-via-api/69104)
- **A blocked-by-default + explicit-exception design exists in court-approved form:** the Anthropic stipulation permits guardrail implementation changes so long as they do not weaken protection — i.e., the obligation is on outcome, not mechanism, leaving room for allowlists — [Tech Policy Press](https://techpolicy.press/analysis-what-anthropics-deal-with-music-publishers-does-and-doesnt-do)

### Inferences
- Implementation shape for the gray zone, each piece grounded above: (a) a **floor** — ignore matches under ~4–8 normalized words (CoTaEval ACS floor; Copilot generic-snippet behavior); (b) a **distinctiveness test** on 8–50-token matches — entropy or corpus-IDF of the matched span (Nasr's entropy requirement is the published mechanism); titles, names, and bibliographic strings are low-entropy/high-frequency and pass; a distinctive turn of phrase is high-IDF and triggers the quote path; (c) a **structural allowlist** for spans the system itself emits as citations (title + author + date patterns), which is safer than frequency alone for a small single-author corpus where even bibliographic strings may be rare; (d) a **quote budget** for explicitly requested quotes — attributed, quotation-marked, and capped well under the publisher ask-permission band (e.g., tens of words per response, far below the 200–400-word prose line), with proportionality to the source piece, since for a *private unpublished* corpus the owner's policy, not fair use, governs; (e) an **owner/role bypass** so the author can retrieve their own text verbatim — the Gemini false-positive record is the cautionary tale for omitting this.
- "User explicitly requested a quote" should route to the attribute path (quote marks + citation + budget check), not to filter exemption: the 2411.01136 finding (compliance rises when text is in context) means user intent is exactly the channel an extraction attack uses.

### Gaps
- No published system documents a working allowlist mechanism for bibliographic/citation strings specifically; this appears to be unpublished engineering practice — the design above is inference.
- No source quantifies a distinctiveness (IDF/entropy) threshold separating "short distinctive phrase" from "common phrase"; it must be calibrated on the corpus itself.
- Fair-use figures above are US/UK publishing practice for published works; nothing retrieved addresses quoting norms for private, unpublished manuscripts (where disclosure itself, not just quantity, is the harm — Harper & Row involved an unpublished manuscript and weighed that heavily, per the FindLaw summary).

## Are there published evals/benchmarks for regurgitation rate in RAG settings?

### Takeaway
Yes — a small but usable 2024–2025 set: CoTaEval (regurgitation from both RAG context and memorization, 7 similarity metrics, win-rate reporting), CopyBench (literal vs non-literal copying rates), the copyright-notice benchmark (43,200 queries, context-compliance focus), CopyBreakRAG (adversarial datastore extraction), and a 2025 multimodal RAG copyright benchmark. The Nasr et al. methodology doubles as an audit recipe (discoverable-memorization testing with suffix-array scoring).

### Cited Findings
- **CoTaEval** (EMNLP/arXiv 2406.18664): evaluates "copyright takedown" methods in two settings — regurgitation from RAG-retrieved context and from fine-tuned memorization — over news and books domains; reports win rates across the 7-metric battery plus utility (blocklisted/in-domain F1) and throughput; headline: no method excels across all metrics — [arXiv:2406.18664](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18664); [v4 HTML with tables](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.18664v4)
- **CopyBench** (EMNLP 2024): literal + non-literal (event/character) copying of copyrighted fiction, with study-guide summaries as the non-infringing reference task; copying scales with model size (literal 0.2%→10.5%, non-literal 2.3%→5.9%, Llama3-8B→70B); authors caution it is "not suitable for making any specific assessments of infringement" — [arXiv:2407.07087](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.07087v1); [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.844/)
- **Copyright-notice benchmark** (arXiv 2411.01136): 43,200 simulated queries testing whether models respect copyright notices on text in user/retrieved context; finds refusal of direct requests but compliance when text is in context even with notices — [arXiv:2411.01136](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.01136v1)
- **CopyBreakRAG** (arXiv 2411.14110): adversarial-query extraction of RAG knowledge bases; >70% of source data extracted from a target application — the datastore-exfiltration side of the regurgitation problem — [arXiv:2411.14110v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.14110v2)
- **Multimodal RAG copyright benchmark** (arXiv 2512.21871, 2025): 50,000 query-content pairs covering copyrighted content recognition in vision-language / multimodal RAG settings — [arXiv:2512.21871v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.21871v1)
- **Extraction-audit methodology reusable as an internal eval:** Nasr et al.'s "discoverable memorization" test — feed the system prefixes of its own corpus and measure exact-suffix completion — is recommended by the authors as the practical audit since it upper-bounds what attackers can extract; on gpt-3.5-turbo (chat) exact suffix completion was 3.5% vs 75% for the instruct model, showing the chat wrapper masks rather than removes the capability — [arXiv:2311.17035](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2311.17035)
- **BloomScrub's evaluation regime** (EMNLP 2025) measures residual long-quote rate after detect-rewrite-abstain, i.e., a certified-guarantee style metric rather than a similarity distribution — [arXiv:2504.16046](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.16046v2)

### Inferences
- For the implementation gate, the usable eval design is: (1) CoTaEval-style scoring — run the 7-metric battery on outputs from a fixed adversarial prompt set (direct "quote this," pointed paraphrase-then-push sequences, style-transfer asks per Ippolito, and CopyBreakRAG-style datastore-dump queries) and report win rates against the no-filter baseline; (2) a discoverable-memorization sweep over the author's corpus (prefix→suffix completion rate) as the regression metric per release; (3) an abstention/false-positive track using owner-legitimate queries (the Gemini failure class) so the gate's over-blocking is measured, not just its leakage.
- All published RAG-regurgitation evals use public copyrighted corpora (news, fiction, GitHub); none evaluates a private single-author corpus, but the mechanics (metrics, prompt families, win-rate reporting) transfer directly since the detector has full corpus access in both cases.

### Gaps
- No standardized public leaderboard or agreed pass threshold for "regurgitation rate" in RAG exists as of the retrieved material — each paper defines its own metric family and reports relative wins.
- No published eval covers multi-turn adversarial sessions or retry-until-leak dynamics (the documented Perplexity and Gemini-retry patterns), only single-shot prompts.
- No 2026 benchmark results for current frontier models were found in these searches; the quantitative anchors are 2024–2025 (Llama2/Llama3/GPT-3.5/4-Turbo era).
