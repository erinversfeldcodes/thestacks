# Embedding Inversion Attacks: Reconstruction Risk and Mitigations for a pgvector Store

Scope: what an attacker who exfiltrates a dense-vector store (1024-dim bge-m3 in Postgres/pgvector HNSW) can recover from the vectors alone, with what fidelity, and what mitigations actually hold up. Current as of November 2026.

## State of the art in text-embedding inversion: fidelity by model, dimension, and length; does the attacker need the exact model?

### Takeaway
Short texts (≲32 tokens) are recoverable near-verbatim from a single dense embedding when the attacker knows or can query the encoder (vec2text: 92% exact match on GTR); fidelity degrades to gist-level for ~128-token chunks, but names and entities survive even there. The 2025–2026 generation of attacks (ALGEN, ZSinvert, Zero2Text) removed the original requirements — per-model trained inverters and thousands of encoder queries — so an exfiltrated vector store must be presumed to leak most of the semantic content of its source text, and for an open model like bge-m3 the attacker is in the strongest possible position (exact encoder in hand, unlimited offline queries).

### Cited Findings
- **Vec2Text (Morris et al., 2023)** frames inversion as iterative controlled generation: generate a hypothesis, embed it, correct toward the target embedding. On 32-token inputs with GTR-base it reaches **BLEU 97.3, token F1 99, 92% exact reconstruction** (50 correction steps + sequence beam search); on OpenAI ada-002/MSMARCO, **BLEU 83.4, 60.9% exact**. The authors conclude embeddings "reveal (almost) as much as text" and should be treated with the same care as raw data — [Text Embeddings Reveal (Almost) As Much As Text, arXiv:2310.06816](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.06816)
- **Fidelity falls with length**: on longer inputs (avg ~81 tokens, up to 128), ada-002 reconstruction drops to BLEU 55.0 and 8.0% exact — still high semantic overlap, no longer verbatim. Across BEIR datasets: Quora (~16 tokens) BLEU 95.5 / token F1 98.6 vs trec-news (~128 tokens) BLEU 14.5 / token F1 51.5. "Reconstruction accuracy inversely correlates with example length"; nothing beyond ~128 tokens was tested — [arXiv:2310.06816](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2310.06816)
- **PII survives even partial reconstruction**: on MIMIC-III clinical notes (32-token, GTR), vec2text recovered **94.2% of first names, 95.3% of last names, 89.2% of full names**, with only 26% of notes exact — entity leakage far exceeds verbatim-text leakage — [arXiv:2310.06816](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2310.06816)
- **Original attack requirements**: vec2text needs black-box query access to the *exact* embedding model (each correction round embeds the hypothesis), plus a per-model trained inversion model built from text–embedding pairs; no gradients or weights needed. Training "does not require access to the embedding model parameters; only the text-embedding pairs" — [arXiv:2310.06816](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2310.06816); [Understanding and Mitigating the Threat of Vec2Text, arXiv:2402.12784](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.12784)
- **A 2025 reproduction study** confirmed the original vec2text claims across multiple datasets — [arXiv:2507.07700](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.07700v1)
- **ALGEN (2025)** drops the per-model-training and mass-query requirements: a closed-form least-squares alignment maps victim embeddings into the attacker's own embedding space, then a generic decoder reconstructs text. **One leaked (text, embedding) pair gives partial inversion; ~30 pairs reach ROUGE-L > 20; 1,000 pairs reach ROUGE-L ~41–46 and cosine ~0.93** across T5, GTR, mT5, mBERT, OpenAI ada-002 and 3-large. Named-entity F1 ~20–24, with organizations, places, and numbers sometimes recovered exactly. Works cross-lingually (EN/FR/DE/ES) at reduced fidelity — [ALGEN, arXiv:2502.11308](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.11308v2)
- **ZSinvert (2025)** is zero-shot: no embedding-specific inversion model, far fewer encoder queries than vec2text, and it remains effective against embeddings perturbed with Gaussian noise up to σ=0.01, though reconstructions are less precise than vec2text's — [Universal Zero-shot Embedding Inversion, arXiv:2504.00147](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.00147v1)
- **Zero2Text (2026)** is training-free, uses recursive online alignment, and claims state-of-the-art fidelity in *strict black-box, cross-domain* settings where prior methods were ineffective — [arXiv:2602.01757](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.01757)
- A 2026 paper framing inversion as **conditional masked diffusion** reports recovering **up to 81.3% of tokens from a single embedding** — [arXiv:2602.11047](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.11047)
- **Fully hidden victim model**: the Transferable Embedding Inversion Attack steals a surrogate encoder from returned representations and attacks the surrogate, with the attack transferring to the victim — exact model knowledge is not required, only query access — [arXiv:2406.10280](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.10280v1)
- **BGE-family models are confirmed targets**: a 2024 study used BGE-Large-en (alongside SimCSE, E5-Large-v2) and found attack accuracy *rises* with more expressive target models, and in-distribution text is easier to reconstruct than out-of-distribution — [arXiv:2404.16587](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.16587v1.pdf)
- **Multilingual models (the bge-m3 class) may be *more* vulnerable**: inversion extends to multilingual encoders, including reconstructing text in languages the attacker's inversion model wasn't trained on, and English-only defenses do not carry over — [Text Embedding Inversion Security for Multilingual Language Models, arXiv:2401.12192](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.12192v3)
- **Invertibility depends on embedding geometry**: mean pooling + cosine similarity is markedly more invertible than CLS pooling + dot product (exact-match 57.9 vs 43.0 on DPR); bottleneck-pretrained encoders (SimLM) are especially vulnerable (exact-match 64.4 zero-shot); reducing dimension 768→256 cuts exact-match 43.0→5.9 at almost no retrieval cost (top-10 0.748→0.731) — [arXiv:2402.12784](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.12784)

### Inferences
- **bge-m3 being an open-weights model puts this deployment in the worst-case threat model.** The attacker downloads the exact encoder, gets unlimited free offline "query access" (the binding constraint in every attack paper), can train a dedicated vec2text-style inverter against it at leisure, and can generate unlimited (text, embedding) pairs — so ALGEN-style alignment needs zero leaked pairs. Nothing about the model is secret.
- **Model identity is not a secret either.** 1024 dimensions narrows the candidate set to a handful of popular open models (bge-m3, bge-large, e5-large, Cohere variants); an attacker can embed probe texts with each candidate and check cosine similarity against stored vectors to fingerprint the encoder in minutes. "The attacker doesn't know it's bge-m3" provides no meaningful protection, and the 2025–2026 black-box attacks (ALGEN, Zero2Text, surrogate-stealing) work even if it did.
- bge-m3 is a high-capacity, high-dimension (1024), multilingual, cosine-similarity encoder — on the wrong side of every invertibility factor identified in arXiv:2402.12784 and arXiv:2404.16587 (expressive model, full dimension, cosine) plus the multilingual-vulnerability finding of arXiv:2401.12192.
- **Realistic recovery estimate for a writing corpus**: chunks embedded at ≲32 tokens → near-verbatim recovery including names; chunks at ~100–500 tokens (typical RAG chunking of prose) → verbatim recovery unlikely with published attacks (none tested beyond ~128 tokens), but high-fidelity gist, named entities (characters, places), distinctive vocabulary, and topic/plot structure should be assumed recoverable per-chunk. For *unpublished creative writing*, gist + character names + plot structure per chunk, across every chunk in order, is close to total confidentiality loss even without verbatim text.

### Gaps
- No published attack evaluates inputs longer than ~128 tokens; fidelity on 256–1000-token chunks is extrapolation (expect gist-level, not verbatim, but no measured numbers).
- No paper was found that inverts bge-m3 specifically (bge-large-en is the closest confirmed target). No reason found to expect it differs from its class.
- Venue/peer-review status of the 2025–2026 attack papers (ALGEN, ZSinvert, Zero2Text, masked-diffusion) is mostly unverified; they are arXiv preprints.

## Membership and attribute inference from embeddings, short of full inversion

### Takeaway
Even where full inversion fails, embeddings support cheaper attacks: recovering a bag of input words, inferring sensitive attributes (including authorship), and testing whether specific text was in the embedded set. These are the fallback risks that remain after partial mitigations blunt verbatim reconstruction.

### Cited Findings
- **Song & Raghunathan (2020), "Information Leakage in Embedding Models"** — the foundational paper: sentence-embedding membership inference scores candidate sentence pairs by embedding similarity (data seen in training/indexing looks more self-similar); extends to aggregate user-level membership by averaging across consecutive sentences; also demonstrates embedding inversion (word-set recovery) and **sensitive attribute inference** against Word2Vec, FastText, GloVe, dual-encoders, and BERT; proposes adversarial-training defenses with modest utility cost — [arXiv:2004.00053](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.00053v2)
- **Mahloujifar et al. (2021)** show black-box membership inference against word embeddings transfers to downstream classifiers and LSTM language models even when the attacker cannot see the embedding layer — [arXiv:2106.11384](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.11384); [Microsoft Research page](https://microsoft.com/en-us/research/?p=762385)
- **GEIA (2023)** moved the field from attribute classification to generative reconstruction of whole sentences, explicitly positioning attribute inference as the weaker predecessor attack — [arXiv:2305.03010](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.03010.pdf)
- A membership-inference survey credits Song & Raghunathan with the first MI attacks on word/sentence embeddings and describes the attack unit as "a sliding window of words or a pair of sentences" — [arXiv:2103.07853](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.07853)

### Inferences
- For a *single-author* corpus, classic membership inference ("was this author's data in the store?") is mostly moot — the store's existence answers it. The relevant sub-inversion threats are: (a) **verbatim-passage confirmation** — an attacker holding a suspected excerpt embeds it with bge-m3 and checks for a near-1.0 cosine neighbor, confirming a specific passage exists in the unpublished corpus (relevant to plagiarism disputes, leak attribution, pre-publication scooping); (b) **attribute/topic inference** — clustering the vectors reveals themes, characters, and corpus structure with no inversion at all; (c) **stylometric authorship signals** embedded in the vectors.
- These attacks need no decoder, no training, and no model queries beyond one embedding call — they are the floor of what any vector exfiltration yields, and noise/quantization levels that defeat vec2text generally do not defeat similarity-threshold tests.

### Gaps
- No found study quantifies membership-inference accuracy specifically against a retrieval/RAG *index* (as opposed to an embedding model's training set); the verbatim-confirmation attack above is an inference from how cosine retrieval works, not a cited experiment.
- No found work on stylometric identification from dense sentence embeddings specifically (authorship attribution from embeddings is plausible but was not located as a measured result).

## Mitigations and their real costs: noise, quantization, dimension reduction, secret transforms, encryption at rest

### Takeaway
Against the original vec2text, cheap defenses looked great: calibrated Gaussian noise, product quantization, or dimension reduction each collapsed reconstruction at ~2% retrieval cost. Against 2025–2026 alignment-based attacks, every one of these — plus local DP, watermarking, dimension shuffling, and secret linear transforms — fails to protect while preserving utility. The only mitigations that survive a known-model attacker are ones that keep vectors (or enough pairs to align them) out of the attacker's hands: real encryption with keys outside the database, and minimizing what is stored at all.

### Cited Findings
- **Gaussian noise (vec2text's own defense)**: at λ=0.01 on GTR, retrieval NDCG@10 drops ~2% (0.302→0.296) while reconstruction BLEU falls 80.4→10.3; the authors call it a sanity check and note the attacker's model wasn't trained on noisy embeddings — [arXiv:2310.06816](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2310.06816). Zhuang et al. found noise λ=0.1 on DPR cuts BLEU 85.3→12.7 and exact 54.6→0 at top-10 retrieval cost 0.748→0.730, and that retraining vec2text on noisy embeddings did *not* restore the attack (exact-match 1.3) — [arXiv:2402.12784](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.12784)
- **…but noise is beaten by newer attacks**: ZSinvert stays effective under Gaussian noise up to σ=0.01 — [arXiv:2504.00147](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.00147v1). BeamClean shows input-independent Gaussian/Laplacian noise (as used in local DP) is vulnerable to adversaries with linguistic priors and embedding knowledge — [BeamClean, arXiv:2505.13758](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.13758v1). Under ALGEN, noise large enough to cut ROUGE-L to ~13 also collapses downstream accuracy (63→30.5 on SNLI); local DP at ε=12 keeps utility but still leaks >25% of non-private ROUGE-L, and ε=1 destroys utility — [arXiv:2502.11308](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.11308v2)
- **Quantization**: product quantization reduced vec2text exact-match to **0.0** with retrieval essentially unchanged (top-10 0.749 vs 0.748 baseline) — [arXiv:2402.12784](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.12784). The 2025 reproduction study likewise found quantization reduces reconstruction while keeping retrieval performance — [arXiv:2507.07700](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.07700v1). ALGEN did **not** test quantization — [arXiv:2502.11308](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.11308v2)
- **Dimension reduction**: 768→256 dims cut vec2text exact-match 43.0→5.9 at top-10 cost 0.748→0.731 — [arXiv:2402.12784](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.12784)
- **Secret transforms**: Zhuang et al.'s defense — a secret, user-specific scaling (f = −2.6·φ(x)), "similar to an API key" — reduced vec2text to BLEU 1.1 / exact 0.0 with retrieval *identical* to baseline, because the same transform applies to all embeddings. The paper does **not** test an attacker who learns the transform or holds known plaintexts — [arXiv:2402.12784](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.12784). ALGEN then showed the generic break: a closed-form least-squares map learned from as few as ~30 (text, embedding) pairs aligns *any* unknown linear view of the embedding space to the attacker's own space; linear-transform watermarking (WET) and dimension shuffling reduced ROUGE-L only marginally (42.9→39.4 and →35.3). The authors conclude "none of the established defense mechanisms" they examined protects embeddings while preserving utility — [arXiv:2502.11308](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.11308v2)
- **Learned/architectural defenses (research-grade)**: Eguard, a mutual-information-trained projection network, increases inversion uncertainty — [AAAI 2025 paper](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/40876/44837); SPARSE (2026) argues uniform-sensitivity DP over-noises and proposes concept-aware elliptical noise targeting privacy-sensitive dimensions — [arXiv:2602.07090](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.07090); STEER transforms queries into an aligned space so providers can't recover query text, avoiding stored-embedding noise — [arXiv:2507.18518](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.18518v2); MESS does private semantic search over perturbed binary embeddings in multi-graph HNSW with defined leakage, noting ORAM/PIR schemes are stronger but carry significant overhead — [arXiv:2607.28999](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.28999)
- **Vendor encryption claims**: IronCore Labs states that encrypting vectors stops inversion attacks and works wherever vectors are stored, provided the developer can transform vectors before storage — [IronCore OWASP 2025 post](https://ironcorelabs.com/blog/2025/owasp-llm-top10-2025-update/). Baffle announced queryable encryption covering pgvector (Nov 2024), keeping embeddings unreadable to DBAs while semantic search still works — [press release](https://manilatimes.net/2024/11/21/tmt-newswire/globenewswire/baffle-announces-vector-database-protection-to-enhance-data-security-for-genai-applications/2009158). These are vendor claims, not independently evaluated here.

### Inferences
- **Decision rule**: mitigations split into (a) *geometry obfuscation* (noise, quantization, rotation, scaling, shuffling, dim-reduction) — cheap, retrieval-friendly, and now demonstrably alignment-attackable given a known model or a few known plaintexts; and (b) *key-based secrecy* (real encryption, keys outside the DB) — robust but incompatible with the DB running the ANN index. With bge-m3 open and public writing by the same author available (an author's *published* work is exactly the known-plaintext an ALGEN attacker would embed), category (a) should be treated as attack-slowing hygiene, not protection.
- Quantization (especially aggressive binary/product quantization) is currently the best *cost-free* hardening: zero measured retrieval loss, zeroes out vec2text, and no published alignment attack against it yet — but absence of a published attack is not evidence of resistance, and ALGEN explicitly left it untested.
- **The small-corpus escape hatch**: all the "vectors must be plaintext for HNSW" pressure assumes the index must live in the database. A single author's corpus is likely 10³–10⁵ chunks; exact brute-force kNN over that many 1024-dim vectors in application memory is milliseconds. The app can store vectors Cloak-encrypted (AES-GCM) in Postgres, decrypt on boot into an in-memory index, and never give the database plaintext vectors at all — eliminating the DB-exfiltration vector-inversion threat entirely at the cost of startup load time and app-memory residency. This is the one architecture where encryption-at-rest and search coexist without vendor products or research crypto.

### Gaps
- No published evaluation of alignment attacks (ALGEN/Zero2Text-class) against product-quantized or binary-quantized stores — the most promising cheap defense is also the least adversarially tested.
- IronCore's and Baffle's schemes (distance-preserving / queryable encryption) were not independently evaluated against alignment attacks in anything found; property-preserving encryption generically leaks the distances it preserves, and no source quantified what that leakage enables.
- No measured numbers found for bge-m3 retrieval degradation under int8/binary quantization specifically (vendor benchmarks exist but weren't verified in this pass).

## Production practice and regulation: are embeddings treated as sensitive/personal data?

### Takeaway
Yes, increasingly and officially: OWASP made "Vector and Embedding Weaknesses" a top-10 LLM risk in 2025 with embedding inversion named explicitly, and pgvector vendor docs say to treat embeddings of sensitive content as sensitive data. On the regulatory side there is no embedding-specific ruling, but the EDPB's Opinion 28/2024 rejects blanket anonymity for AI artifacts derived from personal data (the bar: extraction likelihood must be *negligible*), and practitioner commentary converges on treating embeddings as pseudonymous personal data under the Recital 26 re-identification test — a test embeddings clearly fail given current inversion results.

### Cited Findings
- **OWASP LLM08:2025 "Vector and Embedding Weaknesses"** is a new entry in the OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications 2025, covering embedding inversion ("an attacker with access to embeddings can approximate the original text"), cross-tenant leakage in shared stores, and embedding poisoning; recommended mitigations include permission-aware vector stores with fine-grained access control, separate indexes by sensitivity/role/trust, validation before embedding, retrieval-anomaly monitoring, and limiting embedding retention — [OWASP LLM08:2025](https://genai.owasp.org/llmrisk/llm082025-vector-and-embedding-weaknesses/); [Cobalt analysis](https://www.cobalt.io/blog/vector-and-embedding-weaknesses); [IronCore summary](https://ironcorelabs.com/blog/2025/owasp-llm-top10-2025-update/)
- **EnterpriseDB's pgvector security documentation** states: "Vector embeddings can encode semantic information about the source content. If the source content is sensitive, treat embeddings as sensitive data" — a vendor explicitly instructing users to classify embeddings by their source's sensitivity — [Securing pgvector, EDB docs](https://www.enterprisedb.com/docs/pg_extensions/pgvector/security/)
- A 2026 research paper on vector-DB attacks observes that "standard practice across major vendors is limited to disk-level and transport-level encryption" and query-level encryption of embeddings "is not yet realized in deployed systems" — i.e., production practice lags the stated risk — [arXiv:2604.05480](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.05480)
- **EDPB Opinion 28/2024** (adopted 17 Dec 2024): AI models trained with personal data "cannot, in all cases, be considered anonymous"; anonymity must be assessed case-by-case, and requires that the likelihood of directly or probabilistically extracting personal data of individuals whose data was used be *negligible* — [EDPB Opinion 28/2024 (PDF)](https://edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2024-12/edpb_opinion_202428_ai-models_en.pdf); [EDPB summary](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/news/edpb-opinion-on-ai-models-gdpr-principles-support-responsible-ai_en); [IAPP commentary](https://iapp.org/news/a/edpb-weighs-in-on-key-questions-on-personal-data-in-ai-models)
- The **Hamburg DPA's July 2024 discussion paper** took the opposite view for LLM *weights* (LLMs "do not store personal data," so data-subject rights don't attach to the model itself); IAPP reads the EDPB opinion as disagreeing. Neither document addresses embedding *stores* — [IAPP](https://iapp.org/news/a/edpb-weighs-in-on-key-questions-on-personal-data-in-ai-models); [CMS Law summary of Opinion 28/2024](https://cms.law/en/lux/legal-updates/edpb-opinion-28-2024-key-takeaways-on-processing-personal-data-in-the-context-of-ai-models)
- Practitioner/compliance commentary: embeddings derived from personal data should be handled as **pseudonymous personal data, not anonymous**, because inversion and membership-inference attacks enable re-identification — [hoop.dev, "GDPR compliance for embeddings"](https://hoop.dev/blog/gdpr-compliance-for-embeddings/); a DPO-oriented guide applies the **Recital 26** "means reasonably likely to be used" test to stored embeddings — [GDPR status of stored embeddings](https://si-website-dev.pages.dev/docs/development/gdpr-status-of-stored-embeddings/)
- GDPR-RAG practitioner guidance highlights erasure as the hard operational problem: one subject's data fragments across many vectors; deletion must reach vector stores, agent memory, logs, *and backups/snapshots* (weekly snapshots are commonly missed); mitigations include stripping PII before embedding and keeping an embedding→source-document mapping so erasure completes at next re-index — [dev.to RAG privacy writeup](https://dev.to/tiamatenity/rag-systems-and-privacy-your-vector-database-is-leaking-more-than-you-think-3k1p); [dev.to pipeline-leak writeup](https://dev.to/ijaz18/your-rag-pipeline-is-leaking-customer-data-into-vector-embeddings-37m8); AWS documents a right-to-be-forgotten workflow for Bedrock Knowledge Bases (RAG stores) — [AWS ML blog](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/machine-learning/implementing-knowledge-bases-for-amazon-bedrock-in-support-of-gdpr-right-to-be-forgotten-requests)

### Inferences
- A defensible position for the threat-model doc: **embeddings of the author's unpublished writing are personal data of the author (and of any real persons described in it) and carry the source text's full confidentiality classification.** Given published inversion fidelity, arguing "the vectors are anonymized derivatives" fails both the EDPB negligible-extraction bar and the Recital 26 test; this is the prevailing practitioner view, though not yet settled by any regulator ruling on embeddings specifically.
- Operationally this means: embedding rows must be reachable by the erasure path (delete vectors when source chunks are deleted, including in Neon branch copies and backups), included in export, and kept out of event payloads/warehouse — the same obligations the project already applies to text PII.

### Gaps
- No regulator (EDPB, ICO, CNIL, or a DPA decision) has ruled on vector-store embeddings specifically; everything above the EDPB-opinion level is vendor/practitioner commentary, and the EDPB opinion is about *model weights*, applied to embeddings by analogy.
- No survey data found on what fraction of production vector-DB deployments actually encrypt or access-control embeddings (only the arXiv:2604.05480 claim that deployed practice is disk+transport encryption).

## The layered Postgres posture: what RLS, role grants, and Cloak-encrypted text columns achieve while vectors stay plaintext-indexed

### Takeaway
RLS and least-privilege grants defend against *live, credentialed* over-read (compromised app account, SQL injection, multi-tenant bleed) and nothing else; they are bypassed entirely by the realistic exfiltration paths — superuser/DBA access, a full dump, a backup, a replica, or a Neon branch. If Cloak (AES-GCM, key outside the DB) encrypts the text columns but the `vector(1024)` columns stay plaintext for HNSW, the vectors become the weakest copy of the corpus: a dump attacker gets ciphertext text plus invertible vectors, so Cloak's protection of embedded text is largely voided. Closing that gap means either encrypting the vectors too (moving ANN into the app, viable at this corpus's scale) or accepting and documenting vector inversion as a residual risk of DB compromise.

### Cited Findings
- **Community PostgreSQL has no transparent data encryption**; EDB's pgvector security guidance points to filesystem/block-device encryption (LUKS, encrypted EBS) for at-rest protection — which protects stolen disks, not a live compromised database — [Securing pgvector, EDB docs](https://www.enterprisedb.com/docs/pg_extensions/pgvector/security/)
- **RLS works with HNSW indexes, but it post-filters**: a filtered nearest-neighbor query still traverses the index broadly and applies the policy afterward ("it doesn't restrict the index traversal itself"); EDB recommends partitioning by tenant or partial indexes for strict isolation — [EDB docs](https://www.enterprisedb.com/docs/pg_extensions/pgvector/security/)
- **Standard deployed practice stops at disk- and transport-level encryption**; query-level/field-level encryption of vectors is "not yet realized in deployed systems" outside vendor products — [arXiv:2604.05480](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.05480); Baffle's queryable encryption is the one pgvector-specific field-encryption product found — [Baffle announcement](https://manilatimes.net/2024/11/21/tmt-newswire/globenewswire/baffle-announces-vector-database-protection-to-enhance-data-security-for-genai-applications/2009158); a PostgresConf 2025 talk covers DBA-unreadable embeddings with working semantic search — [PostgresConf session](https://postgresconf.org/conferences/postgresconf_global_2025/program/proposals/secure-semantic-search-with-pgvector)
- **Deleted vectors may linger**: "Ghost Vectors" (2026) studies soft-deleted embeddings remaining reconstructible in HNSW indexes (pgvector HNSW also dead-marks rather than rebuilds on DELETE until vacuum/reindex) — only the paper's setup was readable in this pass, so its quantitative findings are unverified — [arXiv:2606.18497](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.18497)
- OWASP LLM08's mitigation list for stores like this: fine-grained permissioned access, separate indexes by sensitivity, retention limits on embeddings — [OWASP LLM08:2025](https://genai.owasp.org/llmrisk/llm082025-vector-and-embedding-weaknesses/)

### Inferences (applied to The Stacks' stack: Postgres + pgvector HNSW, Cloak available, corpus = one author's unpublished writing)
- **Layer-by-layer honest accounting for the threat-model section:**
  - *Role grants + RLS*: stop a compromised low-privilege credential or injected query from bulk-reading vector rows; worthless against dump/backup/replica/superuser exfiltration. Note the project's own finding that local tests run as superuser, so RLS/grants on new vector tables are untested unless probed with `SET LOCAL ROLE` (project memory: local tests bypass RLS and GRANTs).
  - *Disk/volume encryption (Fly.io, Neon at-rest)*: stops stolen-media attacks only; a live DB compromise or exported branch sees plaintext.
  - *Cloak AES-GCM on text columns, key in app env*: genuinely strong **separate key domain** — a DB-only breach yields unreadable text — but only for data without a plaintext derivative. Embedded chunks have one: their vectors. Inversion (near-verbatim ≤32-token chunks; gist + entities at ~128+) recovers most of what Cloak was protecting. Cloak on text + plaintext vectors ≈ redacting the document but shipping its photocopy.
  - *Neon branches/previews and backups*: copy-on-write branches inherit the vector store wholesale; preview environments and snapshots are additional exfiltration surfaces and must be inside the erasure/retention story (practitioner guidance above flags snapshots as the commonly-missed copy).
- **Recommended honest posture, strongest first**: (1) don't embed what needn't be embedded, and strip direct identifiers pre-embedding where feasible; (2) for this corpus's scale, store vectors Cloak-encrypted and do exact kNN in app memory (or a rebuildable in-process index), removing plaintext vectors from the DB, its backups, and its branches entirely — HNSW-in-Postgres is a scale optimization this corpus doesn't need; (3) if vectors must stay plaintext-indexed, apply aggressive quantization (best currently-unbroken cheap hardening, ~zero retrieval cost) *and* document inversion as a residual risk equal to source-text disclosure under DB compromise; (4) treat noise/rotation/shuffling as non-defenses given an open encoder (ALGEN-class alignment breaks them with a few known plaintexts — and an author's published writing is a ready supply of known plaintexts); (5) keep vectors out of event_log/audit/warehouse exactly as PII, and make erasure delete vector rows *and* reindex/vacuum (ghost-vector risk) across branches.
- A useful one-line framing for the doc: **the vector column inherits the confidentiality classification of the text it encodes, minus verbatim fidelity at long chunk lengths, minus nothing at short ones.**

### Gaps
