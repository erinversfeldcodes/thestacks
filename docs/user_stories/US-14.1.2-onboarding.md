# US-14.1.2 — First-Time Onboarding Flow

## 1. User Story

> **As a** new user who has just registered, **I want** a brief guided introduction that gets my first book onto a shelf and settles my privacy choices **so that** I understand how The Stacks works — and have something to come back to — without feeling overwhelmed or lost.

**What the user wants to accomplish:** Cross the threshold from an empty account to a collection that has begun — one real book placed, and the consent choices the platform needs made honestly and on the record — in a flow short enough that it never feels like paperwork.

**How they accomplish it:** After successful registration and email confirmation, the system launches a lightweight onboarding overlay on the user's first login. The overlay is the **D2 flow** — four steps the reader walks in order: **Welcome → Upload your first book → Consent → Done**. The Upload step routes through the real upload/identify pipeline (US-1.1.1), so the reader leaves onboarding with an actual book on a shelf, not just a tour. The Consent step collects the consent the platform records (`Stacks.GDPR.Consent`), skippable but never a throwaway toggle. Every step is skippable, and skipping persists exactly as advancing does, so the flow never re-triggers.

> **Backend cross-ref**: Issue #149 (complete) replaced the boolean `users.onboarding_completed` with a JSONB `users.onboarding_steps` map (`profile`, `age_verification`, `privacy`) and added `GET /api/onboarding/status` / `PUT /api/onboarding/step/:step`. `onboarding_completed` is now a `GENERATED ALWAYS AS` column derived from those steps. The client flow described below is data-driven (see §2 and §12) and **maps its ordered step descriptors onto this server step model** — each client step that has a server counterpart records completion via `PUT /api/onboarding/step/:step` (and localStorage as the fast client-side guard). See Issue #149 for the canonical server step list; `Stacks.Accounts.onboarding_status/1` and `complete_onboarding_step/2` are the context functions.

**What they see on the page:**
- A full-screen overlay over a softly blurred bookcase — the room the account is about to fill reads faintly through the scrim. A card holds the current step's content, centred, with a row of progress dots beneath it (one dot per step in the sequence, the current one filled). A quiet "Skip" affordance sits away from the primary action at every step.
- **Step 1 — Welcome:** "Welcome to The Stacks" in the elegant serif, tagline "Your personal collection, beautifully organised." A single primary "Get started" button, "Skip" alongside. The tone is an invitation, not a form.
- **Step 2 — Upload your first book:** "Add your first book" heading. This is the *real* first-book upload, not a prompt — the standard upload/identify surface (US-1.1.1) is embedded inline: the reader photographs a cover or barcode, or types an ISBN, and the identified book is confirmed and placed onto a shelf through the same pipeline every later upload uses. On success the card shows the identified title and cover and advances. "Skip" leaves the shelf empty for now.
- **Step 3 — Consent:** "Your privacy choices" heading. Two clearly-described consent items — anonymised usage **analytics**, and the AI **writing-assistant** personalisation — each with plain-language copy and a toggle, defaulting off. The reader is told what each covers and that they can change it any time in Settings. Granting records a timestamp against the choice; skipping records no grant. Neither is forced — "Skip" advances without granting, and persists identically to advancing.
- **Step 4 — Done:** "Welcome to your library" heading, "Your shelves are ready." A single "Start exploring" button that closes the overlay and drops the reader into their collection.

**Acceptance criteria:**
- The flow is the **D2 sequence — Welcome → Upload your first book → Consent → Done** — walked in order, with per-step progress dots.
- **The step sequence is data-driven — an ordered list of step descriptors the view folds over, NOT hardcoded `Welcome -> UploadPrompt -> Complete` branches. A step can be added, reordered, or removed by editing that list alone, without rewriting the flow's update or view logic. This adaptability is an explicit, reviewed acceptance criterion (owner constraint, 2026-08-05): the #318 child is reviewed for it.**
- The Upload step performs a real first-book upload through the US-1.1.1 upload/identify flow (photo or ISBN), not a prompt-only screen; on completion the book is placed on a shelf.
- The Consent step collects analytics + writing-assistant consent per `Stacks.GDPR.Consent`, is skippable, and preserves consent-timestamp semantics (US-8.1.3 / US-8.3) — a grant records its `_at` timestamp; a skip records no grant.
- The flow is fully dismissable at any step via "Skip"; **skipping persists the onboarding state by the same path as advancing** (server step + localStorage), so it never re-triggers.
- Onboarding launches after first registration only (not on subsequent logins), gated on `not onboardingCompleted` and no existing placements.
- Completing (or skipping to the end) leaves the reader in their collection; a book uploaded in step 2 is on its shelf.

## 2. UI Interaction Flow

### Happy Path
1. User logs in for the first time (no placements exist, `onboardingCompleted` is `false` in localStorage).
2. The onboarding overlay renders on top of the current page. The overlay walks an **ordered list of step descriptors** (`Welcome`, `UploadFirstBook`, `Consent`, `Done`); `currentIndex` starts at 0 and progress dots are derived from the list length.
3. **Step 1 — Welcome**: "Welcome to The Stacks" heading, "Your personal collection, beautifully organised." tagline. "Get started" advances (`currentIndex + 1`); "Skip" dismisses.
4. **Step 2 — Upload your first book**: the real US-1.1.1 upload/identify surface embedded inline. The reader takes a photo / enters an ISBN; the book is identified, confirmed, and placed. On a successful placement the step advances; "Skip" advances without a book.
5. **Step 3 — Consent**: analytics + writing-assistant consent items, each with plain-language copy and a toggle (default off). "Continue" advances, recording any granted consent (with timestamp) via `POST /api/gdpr/consent`; "Skip" advances granting nothing.
6. **Step 4 — Done**: "Welcome to your library" heading, "Your shelves are ready." A single "Start exploring" button that finishes the flow and lands the reader in their collection.
7. Progress dots (one per descriptor, so four here) indicate the current step; adding or removing a descriptor changes the dot count automatically.

### Sad Paths
- **Skip at any step**: the flow is marked complete via the **same persistence path as advancing off the last step** — `saveOnboardingCompleted` port + the corresponding `PUT /api/onboarding/step/:step` — the overlay closes, and onboarding never re-triggers. Skipping the Upload step simply leaves the shelf empty; skipping Consent grants nothing.
- **Upload identify fails inside step 2**: the embedded US-1.1.1 surface shows its own error state (see US-1.1.1); the reader can retry or Skip. Onboarding does not advance until a book is placed or Skip is pressed.
- **Consent save fails**: the toggle state is preserved and a retry is offered; the reader may still Skip forward. A failed grant is not recorded (no timestamp written).

### Elm State Machine
- **Page module**: `Components.OnboardingOverlay`
- **Steps as data (the load-bearing design):** the flow is defined by `steps : List Step` — an ordered list of step descriptors the update and view fold over — plus `currentIndex : Int`. There is **no hardcoded `Welcome -> UploadPrompt -> Complete` enum-transition chain**; advancing is `currentIndex + 1`, the current descriptor is `List.getAt currentIndex steps`, and progress dots derive from `List.length steps`. A step is added, reordered, or removed by editing `steps` alone.
- **Model fields involved**: `steps : List Step`, `currentIndex : Int`, `visible : Bool`, plus the embedded child state for the Upload (US-1.1.1) and Consent sub-views.
- **Msg flow**: `NextStep` -> `currentIndex + 1` (clamped; advancing past the last step finishes); `SkipOnboarding` -> finish immediately; step-local messages (`UploadMsg`, `ConsentMsg`) are delegated to the embedded sub-views and can trigger `NextStep` on success.
- **OutMsg pattern**: finishing (advance-off-end or Skip) emits `OnboardingFinished`, which propagates to Main.elm; Main sets `onboardingCompleted = True`, calls `saveOnboardingCompleted ()`, and records the server step(s). Skip and Finish use one identical persistence path.

## 3. API Calls

The onboarding overlay itself is client-side chrome, but two of its steps drive real endpoints:

- **Step 2 (Upload your first book)** calls the standard upload/identify pipeline — `POST /api/upload` (`UploadController.create`) then the confirm/placement flow — exactly as US-1.1.1 describes. This is a real upload, not a prompt: on success a book edition is created/linked and placed on a shelf.
- **Step 3 (Consent)** calls `POST /api/gdpr/consent` (`GDPRController.update_consent/2`) once per granted item, with `{ consent: true, type: "analytics" | "writing_assistant" }`, returning the flags and their `_at` timestamps (see US-8.3). Skipping calls nothing.
- **Step completion** records server-side step granularity via `PUT /api/onboarding/step/:step` (Issue #149), mapping the client's data-driven steps onto the server `onboarding_steps` model; `GET /api/onboarding/status` is the source of truth for whether the flow has run.

The full request/response contracts live in the referenced stories (US-1.1.1 upload, US-8.3 consent) — this story does not restate them.

## 4. Auth & Middleware Guards

- **Auth requirement**: User must be authenticated (overlay only renders when `model.auth` is `Just _`).
- **Plugs fired**: The overlay shell fires none, but its active steps ride the standard authenticated pipelines of the endpoints they call — `POST /api/upload` and the confirm/placement flow (US-1.1.1), `POST /api/gdpr/consent` and `PUT /api/onboarding/step/:step` (`:api` -> `:authenticated`). The current user comes from `Guardian.Plug.current_resource/1` on each.

---

## 5. Database Interactions

The client-side overlay tracks `currentIndex` in memory and mirrors completion to localStorage via ports. Server-side, Issue #149 added an `onboarding_steps` JSONB column on `op.users` and a `GENERATED ALWAYS AS` `onboarding_completed` boolean derived from it; each completed client step maps onto a server step via `PUT /api/onboarding/step/:step`. Beyond that, the two active steps write through their own stories: the Upload step writes `op.books` / `op.book_editions` / `op.bookshelf_placements` (US-1.1.1), and the Consent step writes the `consent_analytics` / `consent_writing_assistant` flags and their `_at` timestamps on `op.users` (US-8.3).

## 12. Elm Frontend State Machine (Detail)

### Route
- **Route variant**: N/A — The onboarding overlay is not route-based. It renders on top of any page when conditions are met.
- **Display condition**: `model.auth == Just _` AND `not model.onboardingCompleted` AND `not model.hasAnyPlacements`

### Init
- **Component init**: `OnboardingOverlay.init` returns `{ steps = defaultSteps, currentIndex = 0, visible = True, ... }`, where `defaultSteps : List Step = [ Welcome, UploadFirstBook, Consent, Done ]`. **The sequence lives in this one list** — reorder, add, or drop a descriptor here and the flow, dots, and progress follow with no other edit.
- **Main.elm init**: On app startup, calls `requestOnboardingStatus ()` port to check localStorage. Also calls `Api.getMyPlacements` to check if user has any existing placements.
- **Port interaction**: `requestOnboardingStatus` port asks JS for localStorage value; JS responds via `onOnboardingStatus` port subscription with a Bool.

### Steps as data (design constraint)
```elm
type Step
    = Welcome
    | UploadFirstBook
    | Consent
    | Done

defaultSteps : List Step
defaultSteps =
    [ Welcome, UploadFirstBook, Consent, Done ]

-- The view folds over `steps`; the update advances by index. No step-to-step
-- transition is hardcoded. currentStep model = List.getAt model.currentIndex model.steps
```
This is an explicit, reviewed acceptance criterion (§1): the sequence is data the flow walks, never a branch chain, so future steps are a one-line list edit.

### Update cycle
- **Msg**: `NextStep` -> `{ model | currentIndex = min (List.length model.steps - 1) (model.currentIndex + 1) }`; if already on the last descriptor, finishes instead (`visible = False`, OutMsg `OnboardingFinished`). No per-descriptor branch — one index bump for every step.
- **Msg**: `UploadMsg subMsg` -> delegated to the embedded US-1.1.1 upload sub-view; a successful placement folds back to `NextStep`.
- **Msg**: `ConsentMsg subMsg` -> delegated to the embedded consent sub-view (toggles + `POST /api/gdpr/consent`); "Continue" folds back to `NextStep`.
- **Msg**: `SkipOnboarding` -> `{ model | visible = False }`, OutMsg `OnboardingFinished` (the identical finish path as advancing off the last step).
- **Main.elm handling**: On `OnboardingFinished`, sets `model.onboardingCompleted = True`, calls `saveOnboardingCompleted ()` port, and records the server step(s) via `PUT /api/onboarding/step/:step`.
- **Main.elm**: `OnboardingStatusReceived completed` -> sets `model.onboardingCompleted = completed`

### View
- **Key elements**: Full-screen overlay with a blurred-bookcase backdrop, a card whose body is `viewStep (currentStep model)` (folded from the descriptor list), and a progress-dot row generated by mapping over `model.steps` with the dot at `currentIndex` marked active.
- **ARIA attributes**: `role="dialog"`, `aria-modal="true"`, `aria-label` reflecting the current step, `tabindex=-1` on the overlay container. (The modality claim — focus trap + Escape — is tracked in #318.)
- **CSS classes**: `onboarding-overlay`, `onboarding-overlay__backdrop`, `onboarding-overlay__card`, `onboarding-overlay__step`, `onboarding-overlay__title`, `onboarding-overlay__tagline`, `onboarding-overlay__actions`, `onboarding-overlay__dots`, `onboarding-overlay__dot`, `onboarding-overlay__dot--active`
- **Button styles**: "Get started" / "Continue" / "Start exploring" use `btn btn--primary`; "Skip" uses `btn btn--ghost`.

## 13. Operational Metrics

- **Onboarding trigger rate**: Percentage of first-time logins where the onboarding overlay renders (should be 100% when `onboardingCompleted == false` and no placements exist).
- **Step progression rate**: Count of users reaching each step (Welcome -> Upload -> Consent -> Done), derived from `currentIndex` transitions and the `PUT /api/onboarding/step/:step` writes.
- **Skip rate per step**: Percentage of users clicking "Skip" at each step. Higher skip rates on early steps indicate poor engagement.
- **First-book upload rate**: Percentage of users who complete a real placement in the Upload step vs skip it — the core value moment of the flow.
- **Consent grant rate per item**: Share of users granting analytics / writing-assistant consent in the Consent step vs skipping (feeds US-8.3 consent metrics).
- **Completion rate**: Percentage of users who reach the Done step and click "Start exploring" vs those who skip at any point.
- **localStorage write success**: Port call `saveOnboardingCompleted` success rate. Failures would cause re-triggering on next login.

---

## 14. Performance & Usability Metrics

- **Onboarding completion vs skip rate**: Primary conversion metric. Target: >50% completion rate. Low completion suggests the flow is too long or not compelling.
- **Time-to-completion**: Elapsed time from overlay render to "Start exploring" click. Indicates engagement level.
- **Time-per-step**: Average time spent on each step. Long dwell on the Upload step may indicate confusion about the upload process.
- **First book placement rate**: Percentage of users who complete onboarding and place their first book within the same session (ties to US-14.1.2 goal).
- **Overlay render performance**: Time from `initPage` to the Welcome step visible. Should be <100ms (the first step is a static render; the Upload and Consent steps carry their own network latency, measured under US-1.1.1 / US-8.3).
- **Re-trigger rate**: How often the onboarding re-appears after being completed (indicates localStorage port failures).

## 15. Cost Tracking

- **Overlay chrome**: The overlay shell (Welcome/Done steps, dots, navigation) is client-side — zero server cost.
- **Upload step**: Inherits US-1.1.1's cost profile when the reader uploads a book (vision identify, Open Library / Google Books lookup, one placement write). Skipped → no cost.
- **Consent step**: One or two single-row `UPDATE`s on `op.users` (per US-8.3) — negligible Neon cost. Skipped → no cost.
- **Step completion**: A small `PUT /api/onboarding/step/:step` write per completed step plus one localStorage write (~100 bytes).
- **Total per-user cost**: dominated entirely by the optional first-book upload; the flow itself is near-zero.
