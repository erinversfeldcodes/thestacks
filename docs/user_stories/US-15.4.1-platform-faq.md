# US-15.4.1 — Platform FAQ

## 1. User Story

> **As a** visitor, an invited beta reader, or someone deciding whether to trust this thing with their library, **I want** a page that answers the practical questions plainly **so that** I do not have to read the source code or email the owner to find out how The Stacks treats my books and my data.

**What they want to accomplish:** Get a straight answer to a specific question — *why won't it add my book? what happens if I delete my account? is this really open source, and where's the code? can I sign up?* — without wading through prose written to set a tone.

**Codebase check — what already exists, and what this story is actually for.** `Page.About` **is built** (`frontend/src/Page/About.elm`, Wave 8 / #318), routed at `/about` (`Navigation/Route.elm`), linked from the top nav (`Main.elm`) and from the signed-out home page, and fully styled (`about__*` rules in `frontend/css/main.css`). It carries the owner's approved launch copy — hero, what it is, the five shelves, what you can do, built to be yours, the wider shelf, closing — plus the transparency links to `/metrics` and `/costs`.

So **this story does not build an About page and must not rewrite one.** About is the *invitation* — voice, conviction, the antilibrary idea. What is missing is the *reference*: there is no `/faq` route, no `Page.Faq` module, and no FAQ content anywhere in the frontend or the router. Three concrete gaps About leaves open:

1. **No answer to "why was my book refused?"** — the platform's single most surprising behaviour is the ISBN hard gate, and About mentions verification only in passing ("every title verified against a real catalogue").
2. **No operational answer on data** — About says "privacy isn't a setting here, it's the default" and "ask to be forgotten and you are, completely", which is the promise. Nobody can find out from it *what* is stored, *what leaves the platform*, or *where the buttons are*.
3. **Nothing links to the source, no licence is named — and the licence contradicts the claim.** ⚠️ Verified twice over:
   - There is **no GitHub link, no repository URL, and no licence mention anywhere in `frontend/src/`**. The repository URL exists only in `README.md` (`https://github.com/erinversfeldcodes/thestacks`).
   - ⛔ **The tracked `LICENSE` is not an open-source licence.** It is `"Source Available License"` — *"Copyright (c) 2026 Erin Versfeld. All rights reserved… made publicly visible for reference and learning purposes only. No other rights are granted."* It explicitly prohibits, without prior written permission, *"using, running, or executing this software for any purpose"*, copying, distribution, and derivative works.
   - So **four** claim sites currently assert something the licence forbids:
     - `frontend/src/Page/About.elm:63` — "The Stacks is an **open-source** reading-management and book-discovery platform"
     - `frontend/src/Page/About.elm:130` — "The Stacks is **open source** and privately hosted, though **you are also welcome to self-host**"
     - `frontend/src/Main.elm:4414` (the footer) — "The Stacks — **open source** book management"
     - `CLAUDE.md:3` — "An **open-source, self-hosted** book management and discovery platform"

     Under the current licence, a reader who accepted the invitation to self-host would be in breach. Option (b) has to restate **all four** — the About page carries the claim twice, in two different sections, so fixing only the "Built to be yours" sentence would leave the contradiction standing three paragraphs above it.

   **This story cannot be built past that contradiction, and must not paper over it.** The FAQ is precisely the page whose job is to make the open-source claim checkable, so writing it forces the resolution. It needs a one-line owner ruling, either direction:
   - **(a) Make the claim true** — relicense to a real open-source licence (AGPL-3.0 is the usual fit for a self-hosted platform that wants modifications shared back). The About and footer copy then stands as written, and the FAQ states the licence and links the repository.
   - **(b) Make the copy true** — keep the source-available licence and change three pieces of copy to say **"source available"** rather than "open source", and drop "you are also welcome to self-host" from About. The FAQ then explains, without embarrassment, that the code is readable but not licensed for use — which is a defensible position, honestly stated.

   Either way the FAQ tells the truth. What it must not do is repeat "open source" while `LICENSE` says all rights reserved. Raise the ruling as a blocking question in the story's DoD; the rest of the page is buildable in parallel.

**How they accomplish it:**
1. The reader follows a link — from the footer, from About, from the 404 page, from the invite-only panel on `/login`, or by typing `/faq`.
2. The page opens with every question already answered — nothing collapsed.
3. They scan the question headings, or use the browser's own find, or arrive on a deep link like `/faq#isbn-gate` which lands on that question.
4. Each answer that concerns something they can *do* links to the actual control — `/settings/privacy`, `/settings/audit-log`, `/costs`, `/metrics`, the repository.

**What they see on the page:**
- A quiet reference page in the same `curator-desk` setting About uses — a librarian's answer sheet rather than a marketing surface. Heading: **"Questions, answered"**, and beneath it one line: "Plainly, and with links to the thing itself wherever there is one."
- A row of six section jumps in the small-caps caption voice: *What this is · Adding books · Your data · Open source · The closed beta · What it costs.*
- Then the sections. Each question is a heading you can link to, in a slightly heavier weight, with the answer set beneath it in body prose. A small chevron control on each question collapses it; a **Collapse all** control sits at the top.
  > **Everything is open by default, deliberately.** An FAQ that makes you click six times to read six answers has optimised for looking tidy over being read — and collapsed text cannot be found by the browser's own search, which is how people actually use a page like this.
- The answers themselves, in the platform's voice but with the hedging removed. For example, under **Adding books**:
  > **Why was my book refused?**
  >
  > Because we could not find its ISBN in Open Library or Google Books, and we will not add a book we cannot identify. This is deliberate and it is not a bug: a library full of half-identified guesses is worse than a smaller one that is right. If the book is real and the catalogues simply do not have it, [enter the ISBN by hand](/upload) — that path skips the photo, not the check.
- Under **Your data**, the answers are specific enough to be checkable, each ending in a link to the control: what is stored, what a data export contains, what erasure actually deletes (and what it anonymises instead, and why), how long uploaded photos are kept (30 days), what leaves the platform and to whom (Open Library, Google Books, Modal, Resend — named), and what is *never* collected.
- Under **Open source**, the repository link and the licence, **named and characterised accurately** — the section that finally makes About's claim checkable, and the one blocked on the ruling above. Whichever way the ruling goes, the answer says what the licence actually permits, in a sentence a person can act on, and links to `LICENSE` in the repository so they can read it themselves. If the ruling is (b), the honest answer is *"the code is public so you can read it and check what we do; it is not licensed for you to run"* — and the self-hosting question is answered "not yet, and here is why" rather than being quietly dropped.
- Under **The closed beta**, an answer that is **accurate at the moment it is read**: when invite-only registration is on, it says so and explains what an invitation is; when it is off, it says registration is open and links to `/login`. It reads the same server flag the login page does (`GET /api/config`), so this page cannot become a stale promise.
- A closing line: "Still stuck? [Tell us](#) — we read everything." linking to the feedback channel.

**Acceptance criteria:**
- `/faq` is a real route rendering a real `Page.Faq` module, reachable from the footer, from `/about`, from the 404 page, and from the invite-only panel on `/login`.
- Every question has a stable `id` and is deep-linkable; arriving on `/faq#isbn-gate` scrolls to and highlights that question.
- All answers are expanded on first render. Collapsing is available and is not the default.
- The ISBN-gate answer, the erasure answer, and the export answer each match the implemented behaviour, and each links to the surface that performs it.
- The repository URL is stated and links to `https://github.com/erinversfeldcodes/thestacks`.
- ⛔ The licence answer matches the tracked `LICENSE` file. **Blocking**: the owner rules on (a) relicense or (b) restate, and the FAQ plus **all four claim sites** (`About.elm:63`, `About.elm:130`, `Main.elm:4414`, `CLAUDE.md:3`) end up saying the same true thing. No placeholder, and no shipping "open source" against an all-rights-reserved licence.
- The closed-beta answer is derived from `inviteOnly` on `GET /api/config`, not hardcoded.
- The page collects nothing, stores nothing, and requires no authentication.
- Zero new orphaned CSS classes: every `faq__*` class literal has a rule in `frontend/css/main.css` in the same change.

## 2. UI Interaction Flow

### Happy Path
1. Reader clicks "Questions" in the footer, or navigates to `/faq`.
2. `Main.initPage` matches `Route.Faq` → `PageFaq (Faq.init { fragment = url.fragment })`.
3. The page renders all sections with all answers open. If `fragment` names a known question, that question gets the `faq__question--targeted` treatment and `Browser.Dom.getElement` + `setViewport` scrolls it into view below the fixed nav.
4. Reader reads, and follows a link out to the control an answer describes.

### Interaction: collapsing
1. Reader clicks a question's chevron → `ToggledQuestion id` → the id moves in or out of `collapsed : Set String`.
2. Reader clicks **Collapse all** → `ClickedCollapseAll` → every id enters the set; the control becomes **Expand all**.
3. State is per-visit and not persisted. A remembered collapse state means a reader returns to a page that looks broken and does not know why.

### Sad Paths
- **Unknown fragment** (`/faq#nonsense`): the page renders normally with nothing targeted. No error, no scroll. A bad anchor is not worth a message.
- **`GET /api/config` fails or has already failed**: the closed-beta answer falls back to the conservative wording — "New accounts are opened by invitation at the moment" — matching the login page's fail-closed treatment of the same flag. The rest of the page is unaffected; a config blip must not blank an information page.
- **JavaScript disabled**: the SPA does not render at all, here as everywhere. Out of scope for this story, but worth stating: an FAQ is the page most defensibly served as static HTML, and if the platform ever wants a no-JS surface this is the first candidate. Recorded, not built.
- **Deep link from an external site into a question that has since been renamed**: question ids are part of the page's public contract. Once published, an id is kept even if the wording changes; if a question is genuinely retired, keep the id and answer it with a pointer. Note this in the module doc so a later copy edit does not quietly break someone's bookmark.

### Elm State Machine
- **Page module**: `Page.Faq`
- **Model fields involved**: `collapsed : Set String`, `targeted : Maybe String`
- **Msg flow**: `ToggledQuestion String` · `ClickedCollapseAll` · `ClickedExpandAll` · `NoOpScrolled` (the `Task.attempt` result from the fragment scroll)
- **RemoteData states**: none — the page fetches nothing. The one piece of server state it reads (`inviteOnly`) is already in `Main`'s model and is passed in as a view argument.
- **OutMsg pattern**: `NoOut` throughout. The FAQ changes nothing.

## 3. API Calls

**None of its own.** The page makes no request.

### `GET /api/config` (already fetched by `Main`)
- **Auth**: None
- **Pipeline**: `:api`
- **Controller**: `StacksWeb.ConfigController.show/2`
- **Response**: `{ "ageGatingEnabled": bool, "inviteOnly": bool }`
- **Use here**: `Main` already fetches this at boot for age-gating (ADR-020) and, after US-14.1.3, for the registration gate. `Page.Faq.view` receives `inviteOnly` as an argument so the closed-beta answer states what is currently true. **No second fetch** — a page that re-fetches config it has already got is a page that can disagree with the rest of the app.

## 5. Database Interactions

**None.** No reads, no writes, no tables.

### GDPR
No personal data is created, read, or transmitted. The page collects nothing — no email capture, no "was this helpful?" telemetry, no analytics event per question opened. It is the one surface on the platform where the honest implementation is to instrument nothing, and instrumenting an *FAQ about privacy* would be a small joke at the reader's expense.

The FAQ nonetheless carries GDPR obligations in its **content**: it is where the platform's data practices are stated in language a person can read, which is what "transparent" means in Article 12. The answers under **Your data** must be checked against the implementation, not against intent:

| Answer | Must match |
|---|---|
| "what a data export contains" | `Stacks.GDPR.Export.export_user_data/2` — including its deliberate exclusions (password hash, tokens, raw embedding vectors, image bytes and storage paths) |
| "what erasure deletes" | `Stacks.GDPR.Deletion.delete_user_data/2` — and it must say honestly that comment bodies are **anonymised in place** rather than deleted, so replies hanging off them survive, and that `event_log` entries are **redacted** rather than removed |
| "how long photos are kept" | the 30-day `expires_at` on `op.uploaded_images` and the retention sweep |
| "what leaves the platform" | the actual outbound services: Open Library, Google Books, Modal (vision), Resend (email), Cloudflare R2 (image storage), and — where a reader has opted in — the writing-assistant model provider |
| "what you consent to separately" | the consent flags on `op.users` and the `ConsentCheck` plug |

⚠️ **These answers go stale silently.** Add a line to the `gdpr-review` skill's checklist: a change to `GDPR.Export`, `GDPR.Deletion`, the retention window, or the set of external services must check whether `Page.Faq`'s answers still tell the truth. A wrong FAQ answer about erasure is a compliance defect wearing the costume of a copy edit.

## 12. Elm Frontend State Machine (Detail)

### Route
- **Route variant**: `Route.Faq` — new. Add to the type, to `parser` (`Parser.map Faq (s "faq")`), and to `toPath` (`"/faq"`) in `frontend/src/Navigation/Route.elm`.
- **URL**: `/faq`, with `#question-id` fragments
- **Public or authenticated**: public

### Init
- **`initPage` branch**: `Route.Faq → ( PageFaq (Faq.init url.fragment), scrollToFragmentCmd )`
- **API calls on init**: none
- **Initial model state**: `collapsed = Set.empty` (everything open), `targeted = url.fragment |> Maybe.andThen knownQuestionId`
- ⚠️ `Main`'s `initPage` currently receives the route, not the URL, in some branches — confirm the fragment is available at the branch, and thread it if not. A fragment silently dropped one layer up is precisely the chain-break that leaves a deep link "built but not wired".

### Model
```
{ collapsed : Set String
, targeted : Maybe String
}
```

### Content structure
Copy lives in the module, one function per section, in the About page's pattern — so a copy edit is a local change to one block rather than a re-layout:
```
type alias Question =
    { id : String        -- part of the page's public contract; never reused, never removed
    , question : String
    , answer : List (Html Msg)
    }

sections : { inviteOnly : Bool } -> List ( String, String, List Question )
    -- ( sectionId, sectionTitle, questions )
```
Question ids to publish (each becomes a citable anchor): `what-is-this`, `who-runs-it`, `isbn-gate`, `manual-isbn`, `duplicate-editions`, `five-shelves`, `what-we-store`, `data-export`, `erasure`, `photo-retention`, `who-sees-my-shelves`, `what-leaves-the-platform`, `open-source`, `self-hosting`, `licence`, `closed-beta`, `invitations`, `running-costs`, `how-its-funded`.

### Update cycle

| Msg | Model change | Cmd | OutMsg |
|-----|-------------|-----|--------|
| `ToggledQuestion id` | `collapsed` gains or loses `id` | None | `NoOut` |
| `ClickedCollapseAll` | `collapsed = allQuestionIds` | None | `NoOut` |
| `ClickedExpandAll` | `collapsed = Set.empty` | None | `NoOut` |
| `ScrollAttempted _` | — | None | `NoOut` |

`scrollToFragmentCmd` is `Browser.Dom.getElement id |> Task.andThen (\e -> Browser.Dom.setViewport 0 (e.element.y - navOffset)) |> Task.attempt ScrollAttempted`. The `navOffset` exists because the top nav is sticky and a naive anchor jump puts the targeted question underneath it.

### View
- **Key elements**: `viewHeader`, `viewSectionJumps`, `viewCollapseAllControl`, `viewSection`, `viewQuestion`.
- **Each question** renders as a `button` carrying `aria-expanded`, `aria-controls` pointing at its answer's id, and the question `id` on the wrapping element so the fragment resolves. The answer is a `div` with `hidden` applied when collapsed — present in the DOM in the open (default) case, which is what makes browser find work.
- **ARIA**: the section-jump row is a `nav` with `aria-label="Sections"`. The targeted question receives `aria-current="true"` as well as its visual treatment, so a screen-reader user knows where the deep link landed.
- **Keyboard**: every question control is a real `button`, so tab order and Enter/Space work without handlers. No custom key trapping.
- **CSS classes**: `page--faq`, `faq__lede`, `faq__jumps`, `faq__jump`, `faq__collapse-all`, `faq__section`, `faq__section-title`, `faq__question`, `faq__question--targeted`, `faq__question-button`, `faq__chevron`, `faq__answer`, `faq__answer-link`, `faq__closing`.
  ⚠️ **All fourteen need rules in `frontend/css/main.css` in the same change.** Three surfaces have already shipped fully unstyled on this project because the markup named classes that had no rule — a defect class no test catches, since the classes *are* present. Re-run the Elm-class-literal set difference after adding the component and confirm you added **zero** orphans; `scripts/check-css.sh` gates it. The `curator-desk` and several `about__*` rules already exist and should be reused rather than duplicated where the treatment is identical.
- **Test ids**: `faq-page`, `faq-jumps`, `faq-collapse-all`, and `faq-question-<id>` per question — the per-question id lets an E2E spec assert a specific answer exists rather than counting elements.

### Changes to existing surfaces (small, and each belongs to its own story — cross-reference, do not absorb)
- **Footer** (`Main.viewFooter`, US-15.3.1): currently one line of text, `"The Stacks — open source book management"`. Add two links beside it — **Questions** (`/faq`) and the **repository** — which makes the footer's own claim checkable for the first time. ⚠️ If the ruling is (b), the footer's wording changes here too; the line is one of the three surfaces the ruling covers.

Do not otherwise restructure the approved copy. ⚠️ The exception is the ruling: if it is (b), **two** sentences in this file change — `About.elm:63` ("an open-source reading-management and book-discovery platform") and `About.elm:130` ("open source and privately hosted, though you are also welcome to self-host") — and those are copy edits the owner signs off on, not ones this story makes unilaterally.

## 13. Operational Metrics

Deliberately thin, and that is the finding rather than an omission. The page has no endpoint and emits no events, so the only signals available are the ones the platform already collects.

- **SPA route views for `/faq`**: from whatever page-view counting `Main` already does. If none exists, do **not** add per-question instrumentation for this story — see §5.
- **Outbound click-through from FAQ answers**: not instrumented, and not worth instrumenting. The proxy signal that matters is the one below.
- **Feedback volume by category** (US-15.5.1): a fall in "Something's confusing" submissions after the FAQ ships is the real measure of whether it works. A rise in questions the FAQ already answers means the page is not being found — a navigation problem, not a content one.
- **Support email volume**: the owner's own inbox, counted by hand during a closed beta. Honest, and sufficient at this scale.
- **Answer-accuracy check**: a CI assertion, not a runtime metric — a test that fails when `GDPR.Export`'s exported key set or `GDPR.Deletion`'s step list changes without a corresponding touch to `Page.Faq`. Cheap, and it catches the one defect that matters here (§5).

## 14. Performance & Usability Metrics

- **Bundle size delta**: the page is static copy plus a `Set` — expect single-digit KB in `app.js`. Measure it; an FAQ that measurably slows every page load for every reader would be paying the wrong price.
- **Time to interactive**: no change. The page fetches nothing and blocks on nothing.
- **Deep-link accuracy**: an E2E assertion rather than a metric — `/faq#erasure` must scroll the erasure question fully clear of the sticky nav. ⚠️ Assert on the *question's* position, not on the presence of the fragment in the URL; the URL fragment is set by the browser whether or not the scroll worked.
- **Findability**: share of readers reaching `/faq` from each entry point (footer, About, 404, login panel). If the 404 route is a meaningful share, the FAQ is doing work the navigation should be doing.
- **Scroll depth**: whether readers reach the last section. If almost nobody does, the section order is wrong — the ISBN-gate and data answers should be near the top, since those are the questions people arrive with.
- **Repeat visits**: a reader returning to `/faq` more than twice in a week is a reader who could not find the answer. That is the clearest usability failure signal this page can produce.

## 15. Cost Tracking

- **Compute**: zero. No endpoint, no query, no job.
- **Bandwidth**: the page's content is part of `app.js`, already served to every visitor. A few KB, once, cached.
- **External services**: none.
- **Total**: **effectively zero**, which is the argument for building it first. Of everything in Milestone B, this is the cheapest surface that changes whether a stranger trusts the platform — and its real value is not the page but the ruling it forces: building it is what surfaced that the shipped "open source" copy contradicts the tracked `LICENSE`. A defect found by writing a FAQ answer is the cheapest kind there is.
