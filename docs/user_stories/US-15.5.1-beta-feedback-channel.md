# US-15.5.1 — Beta Feedback Channel

## 1. User Story

> **As a** beta reader, **I want** an obvious way to tell the person who built this that something is broken, confusing, or missing **so that** I do not have to find their email address to be useful — and **I want to see exactly what gets sent** before I send it.

> **As the** platform owner, **I want** feedback to arrive in one place with enough context to act on **so that** a closed beta produces signal rather than a scattering of half-remembered complaints.

**What they want to accomplish:** Close the loop between "this is wrong" and someone knowing about it, in one interaction, without losing their place on the page and without handing over anything they did not agree to hand over.

**How they accomplish it:**
1. The reader clicks **Tell us** — in the footer on every page, and in the user menu dropdown when signed in.
2. A panel opens over the page. Their place is kept; nothing navigates.
3. They pick what kind of thing it is, write the message, and — before the send button — read the short list of what will accompany it.
4. They send. The panel confirms, and closes itself.

**What they see on the page:**
- A centred panel in the platform's card treatment (`feedback-modal`), headed **"Tell us"**, with one line beneath: "You're one of a small number of people using this while it's still being built. What you notice is the most useful thing we have."
- **What kind of thing is it?** — four radio options, worded as a reader would say it rather than as a tracker would file it:
  - *Something's broken*
  - *Something's confusing*
  - *An idea*
  - *Something else*
- **What happened?** — a textarea, autofocused, with the placeholder "As much or as little as you like." A live character count appears only once past 4,500, and only as a caution, not a scold.
- Then the part that makes this honest — a bordered, **expanded** block headed **What we'll send with this**, listing every item in plain words:
  > - What you wrote, and which of the four kinds you picked
  > - The kind of page you were on — *the bookshelf page* — not the page's address, and never anyone else's name or handle
  > - Which build of The Stacks you're using
  > - That it was you, because you're signed in — so we can write back
  >
  > And what we do **not** send: no screenshot, no copy of the page, no record of what you clicked, no browser fingerprint, nothing about anyone else. Nothing is collected in the background — this list is the whole of it.
- **May we write back about this?** — a tickbox, defaulted **off**. Signed in, ticking it means "reply to my account email"; signed out, it reveals a single optional email field.
- Two controls: **Send** (disabled until the message reaches ten characters) and **Not now**.
- Footnote in the caption voice: "Kept for six months while the beta runs, then deleted. You can ask for yours sooner — [your data](/settings/privacy)."

**What they see after sending:**
- The panel body is replaced, in place, by a short acknowledgement: **"Read and filed."** — "Thank you. If it's a bug, it's now on a list with a person looking at it." The panel closes itself after four seconds, or on any click.
- No ticket number. A number implies a workflow and an SLA that a one-person closed beta does not have, and inventing one would be the small dishonesty this whole surface exists to avoid.

**Acceptance criteria:**
- The affordance is reachable from every page, signed in or out, without leaving the page.
- A signed-out visitor can submit. The login page is where a beta invitee is most likely to hit a wall, and a feedback channel that requires an account cannot hear about it.
- The disclosure list is expanded by default and enumerates every captured field. Nothing is captured that is not on the list.
- The captured page context is the route **pattern** (`/u/:handle`), never the concrete path — no ids, no handles, no other reader's name.
- No screenshot, no DOM capture, no console log, no click trail, no raw user-agent string, no session replay.
- The owner's notification email carries a **link and a category**, never the message body.
- The event payload carries no message body and no email address.
- Erasing a user deletes their submissions outright, message body included.
- Anonymous submissions are purged after 180 days by a scheduled sweep, and the form says so.
- A failed send preserves the draft.

## 2. UI Interaction Flow

### Happy Path
1. Reader clicks **Tell us** in the footer → `Main` receives `OpenedFeedback`, sets `feedback = Just (FeedbackModal.init { route = currentRoute, isSignedIn = … })`.
2. Panel renders; focus moves to the textarea; the page behind is inert (focus trapped, `Escape` closes).
3. Reader picks a category (`CategoryPicked`) and types (`MessageChanged`).
4. **Send** enables at ten characters. Reader clicks → `Submitted` → `Api.submitFeedback payload`, `submitState = Loading`, button shows `spinner--small`.
5. HTTP 201 → `submitState = Success` → the acknowledgement body renders, and a 4s `Process.sleep` schedules `AutoDismissed`.
6. `AutoDismissed` → `Main` clears `feedback`; focus returns to the **Tell us** control that opened it.

### Route fallback
`/feedback` renders the same component full-page rather than as an overlay, so the affordance survives being linked to from outside the app — an email, the FAQ, a message from the owner. `Components.FeedbackModal` takes a `presentation : Overlay | Page` argument; one component, two framings, no second implementation to drift.

### Sad Paths
- **Message under ten characters**: **Send** stays disabled. No error text — a disabled button next to an empty box needs no explanation.
- **Message over 5,000 characters**: the count turns amber and Send disables with "That's longer than we can take — 5,000 characters. Could you send the shorter version, or two?"
- **Rate limited** (429): "You've sent a few already — thank you, genuinely. Try again in a little while." ⚠️ The draft is **kept**, and the panel does not close. Discarding someone's third bug report of the afternoon as a reward for diligence would be the worst possible failure here.
- **Network failure / offline**: `submitState = Failure` → "That didn't get through. Your message is still here — try again when you're back online." The draft persists in the model. The shell's connectivity banner (which sits above the nav) is already telling them why.
- **Server error (500)**: same treatment as a network failure. The reader's job is not to distinguish our failures.
- **Invalid optional email**: client-side validation on the field; Send disables with "That doesn't look like an email address. You can leave it blank."
- **Reader closes the panel with text in it**: a confirm step — "Discard what you've written?" with **Discard** and **Keep writing**. One of the few places a confirm is warranted, because the alternative silently destroys the only copy.
- **Signed out with the tickbox on and no email**: Send disables. The tickbox promises a reply; without an address it cannot be kept.

### Elm State Machine
- **Component module**: `Components.FeedbackModal`, owned by `Main` (so it can open over any page) with a `Route.Feedback` page framing
- **Model fields involved**: `category`, `message`, `wantsReply`, `contactEmail`, `contactEmailValidation`, `submitState`, `confirmingDiscard`
- **Msg flow**: `CategoryPicked → MessageChanged → ToggledWantsReply → ContactEmailChanged → Submitted → GotSubmitResponse → AutoDismissed`
- **RemoteData states**: `submitState : RemoteData Http.Error ()`
- **OutMsg pattern**: `CloseRequested` and `Submitted` propagate to `Main`; `Main` owns opening, closing, and focus restoration. The component never navigates.

## 3. API Calls

### `POST /api/feedback`
- **Auth**: **Optional** — `:optional_auth`
- **Pipeline**: `:api`, `:optional_auth`, `:rate_limit_feedback`
- **Controller**: `StacksWeb.FeedbackController.create/2`
- **Request body**:
  ```json
  {
    "category": "broken" | "confusing" | "idea" | "other",
    "message": "…",
    "route_pattern": "/bookshelf/:name",
    "platform": "desktop-chromium",
    "app_version": "…",
    "contact_email": "…" | null
  }
  ```
- **Response (success)**: `{ "message": "received" }` — HTTP 201. Deliberately **not** the created row: echoing the submission back gives the client nothing and turns the response into a second copy of the body in a log somewhere.
- **Response (error)**: `{ "errors": { "message": ["is too short"] } }` 422 · `{ "error": "rate_limited" }` 429
- **Server-side authority over the payload**: `app_version` is taken from the **server's** build stamp, not the client's claim. `platform` is validated against a closed allowlist of coarse labels and anything unrecognised is stored as `"unknown"` — the field exists to help reproduce a bug, and an unbounded client-supplied string is both a fingerprint vector and an unbounded metric label. `route_pattern` is validated against the set of patterns `Route` can produce and rejected otherwise, which is what stops a client from smuggling a concrete path (with its ids and handles) into the field.

### `GET /api/admin/feedback`
- **Auth**: Admin + MFA
- **Pipeline**: `:api`, `:admin`, `:rate_limit_admin`
- **Controller**: `StacksWeb.FeedbackAdminController.index/2`
- **Query params**: `status` (`new`|`triaged`|`closed`), `category`, `limit`, `offset`
- **Response**: `{ feedback: [{ id, category, message, route_pattern, platform, app_version, status, submitter: { handle, email } | null, contact_email, created_at }], total }` — HTTP 200
- ⚠️ Every call is audited by `AuditAdminCall`. This endpoint returns other people's writing; the audit entry is what makes reading it accountable.

### `PUT /api/admin/feedback/:id/status`
- **Request body**: `{ "status": "triaged" | "closed" }`
- **Response**: `{ feedback: { id, status, triaged_at } }` — HTTP 200

## 4. Auth & Middleware Guards

- **Plugs fired (submit)**: `SecurityHeaders` → `AuthPipeline` in optional mode → `RateLimiter(bucket: :feedback)`.
- **New rate-limit bucket `:feedback`**: **5 requests / 3600 seconds**, keyed on the **trusted client IP** (the `Fly-Client-IP` / trusted-proxy resolution the limiter already implements, falling back to `conn.remote_ip`).
  - Why per-IP and not per-user: the endpoint accepts anonymous submissions, so a per-user key has nothing to key on for exactly the case that most needs protecting. A free-text sink open to unauthenticated callers is a spam target and a storage-cost target.
  - Why an hour and not a minute: five *considered* reports in an hour is generous for a real reader and tight for a script. The window is what makes the limit meaningful; a 5/minute limit would be no limit at all.
  - ⚠️ Reuse the existing bounded-label telemetry — `stacks_rate_limit_rejected_count_total{bucket="feedback"}`, with the IP never used as a label.
- **No consent gate.** `ConsentCheck` is not applied and should not be: the reader is volunteering a message, and the act of pressing Send *is* the consent. Interposing a consent dialogue in front of a feedback form would be theatre. The **optional contact email** is the one piece of newly-solicited personal data, and it is guarded by its own explicit, default-off tickbox — which is the consent, recorded by the field's presence.
- **Visibility checks**: N/A — feedback is not content on the platform and appears nowhere public.
- **Age gate**: N/A.
- **Ownership checks (admin)**: `:admin` + `RequireMFA` + `AuditAdminCall`. Not `:require_owner` — triaging feedback is exactly the work a future non-owner admin should be able to do, unlike widening the beta (US-14.1.3).

## 5. Database Interactions

### Write: New table `op.feedback_submissions`
- **Proto source**: add `FeedbackSubmission` to a new `proto/stacks/common/v1/feedback.proto`, register it in `proto/persisted.exs`, run `mix proto.sync`. `git add` the generated migration immediately — `mix test` deletes untracked `_add_*` migrations.
- **Columns**:
  - `id` (uuid PK)
  - `user_id` (uuid FK `op.users`, **ON DELETE CASCADE**, nullable)
  - `category` (text, NOT NULL)
  - `message` (text, NOT NULL) — ⚠️ **free-text PII**
  - `route_pattern` (text, nullable)
  - `platform` (text, nullable)
  - `app_version` (text, nullable)
  - `contact_email` (citext, nullable) — ⚠️ **personal data, and for anonymous senders it is unreachable by erasure**
  - `status` (text, NOT NULL, default `"new"`)
  - `triaged_by_id` (uuid FK `op.users`, ON DELETE SET NULL)
  - `triaged_at` (timestamptz, nullable)
  - `created_at` (timestamptz, NOT NULL)
- **⛔ `ON DELETE CASCADE` on `user_id`, not `SET NULL`.** `SET NULL` is the exact shape of the #185 comment-body leak: it nulls authorship and leaves the body — the reader's own words — sitting in the table after they have asked to be forgotten. Feedback has no thread structure to preserve (unlike `op.post_comments`, which had to be anonymised in place so replies did not orphan), so the correct erasure is **deletion**, and the FK should express that.
- **Indexes**: `feedback_submissions_status_created_at_index` (`status`, `created_at DESC`) — the triage queue; `feedback_submissions_user_id_index` — the erasure step and the export.
- **Changeset validations**: `category` inclusion in `~w(broken confusing idea other)`; `message` length 10..5000; `platform` inclusion in the coarse allowlist or `"unknown"`; `route_pattern` inclusion in the known-pattern set; `contact_email` format when present; `status` inclusion in `~w(new triaged closed)`.

### Read: Triage queue
- **Table(s)**: `op.feedback_submissions` LEFT JOIN `op.users`
- **Query**: filter by `status` and `category`, order `created_at DESC`, limit/offset. Served by the composite index.

### GDPR: erasure + export reachability
This is the story's highest-risk section: it introduces a free-text PII sink reachable by unauthenticated callers. Run the `gdpr-review` skill on the diff.

- **Erasure** (`GDPR.Deletion.delete_user_data/2`) gains one step, ordered **before** `:delete_user`:
  - `:delete_feedback_submissions` — `delete_all` on `op.feedback_submissions where user_id == ^user_id`. Belt to the FK cascade's braces, for the same reason `:delete_uploaded_images` is explicit: it makes the guarantee independent of cascade timing and gives `Stacks.Release.do_erase/2`'s operator summary a count to report.
  - Also null `triaged_by_id` where the erased user was the triager — handled by that FK's `SET NULL`, which is correct here because it is a reference and not content.
  - ⚠️ Add it to the **`op.users` schema-sweep guard**'s expectations in `test/stacks/gdpr_test.exs`. The guard walks `op.users` columns and will not see this table; the explicit test is the guarantee. Assert on the **message body**, not just on the row count — a test that only counts rows would pass against a `SET NULL` implementation.
- **Export** (`GDPR.Export.export_user_data/2`) gains a `feedback_submissions` key: `{ category, message, route_pattern, created_at, status }`. The reader's own words are theirs; the export must carry them. Deliberately excluded: `triaged_by_id` and `triaged_at` — internal handling state, not their personal data.
- **Anonymous submissions cannot be erased on request.** A person with no account has no erasure request to make, and no way for the platform to identify their rows if they did. So they must go on a clock: `FeedbackRetentionJob` deletes submissions older than **180 days**, which spans the closed beta and matches what the form promises. This is the same reasoning as the 30-day image retention window and the `ExpiredInvitesSweepJob` — where a subject cannot exercise erasure, retention must be bounded by default rather than by request.
- **Third parties in the message body.** A reader writing "the block button didn't work when I tried to block @someone" has put another person into the field. Nothing can prevent that, and no validation should try. The mitigations are containment: the body never enters `event_log`, never enters the warehouse, never enters a notification email, and is reachable only through an MFA-gated, audited admin endpoint. Say so in the disclosure — "nothing about anyone else" is a statement about what *we* capture, and it should not be read as a promise about what the reader types.
- **Not written to `event_log`**: `message`, `contact_email`, and the submitter's email. See §6. Add the assertion to `Stacks.Events.PayloadContract`.

## 6. Event Flow & Lifecycle

### Events Emitted

#### `feedback.submitted`
- **Event type**: `feedback.submitted`
- **Aggregate**: `feedback` / feedback_id
- **Payload**: `%{category: "broken" | "confusing" | "idea" | "other", authenticated: boolean}`
- **Emitted by**: `Stacks.Feedback.submit/1`
- **Emission method**: `Events.emit_safe/1`
- ⚠️ **No message, no email, no route, no user id.** `event_log` is immutable except for GDPR redaction, so a body written there is a liability for the life of the platform — and the `:scrub_event_log` step can only redact payloads it knows reference a user, which an anonymous submission does not. `authenticated` is a boolean, deliberately, so the counter is useful without naming anyone.

#### `feedback.triaged`
- **Aggregate**: `feedback` / feedback_id
- **Payload**: `%{status: "triaged" | "closed"}`
- **Emitted by**: `Stacks.Feedback.set_status/3`

### Event Handlers Triggered

- **Handler**: `Stacks.Notifications.FeedbackHandler` (new)
- **Listens for**: `feedback.submitted`
- **Action**: enqueues an `EmailDeliveryJob` to the platform owner with a new template `feedback_received`.
- **⛔ The notification email carries a link and a category. Not the body.**
  > "A new *Something's broken* report arrived. Read it: `https://<host>/admin/feedback?status=new`"

  **The decision, and the alternative that was rejected.** Putting the message body in the email would be more convenient — the owner could triage from their phone without signing in. It would also mean every reader's words, possibly naming third parties, leaving the platform into Resend's infrastructure and then into a mail provider's storage, indefinitely, outside the reach of `delete_user_data/2`. That is a straightforward erasure hole created for a convenience. The link keeps the body inside the platform behind MFA, and it is the same reasoning that keeps a fetchable storage pointer out of the GDPR export.
- **Downstream effects**: one email per submission, subject to `EmailDeliveryJob`'s existing per-user (10/hour) and global (100/hour) limits. ⚠️ Those limits are keyed per *recipient*, and the recipient here is always the owner — so a burst of feedback could hit the per-user ceiling and drop notifications. Coalesce instead: the handler should skip the email if one was already sent for a `new`-status submission within the last 15 minutes. The admin queue is the system of record; the email is only a nudge, and a dropped nudge should not look like a dropped report.

### Audit Log
- `Audit.log(admin_id, "feedback.read" | "feedback.triaged", resource_type: "feedback", resource_id: id, ip: …)`. Reading someone's feedback is an access to their personal data and belongs in the audit trail that US-8.5 renders.

## 7. Background Jobs (Oban)

### `Stacks.Workers.EmailDeliveryJob` (existing)
- **Queue**: `:notifications`
- **Args**: `%{"template" => "feedback_received", "user_id" => owner_id, "params" => %{"category" => category, "admin_url" => url}}`
- ⚠️ **`params` must not carry the body.** The args of an Oban job are persisted in `oban_jobs` and survive job completion for the retention period — a second, unaudited copy of the message in a table no erasure step touches. Add `oban_jobs` to the zero-row sweep when verifying this story: a `feedback_received` job whose args contain a `message` key is the defect.

### `Stacks.Workers.FeedbackRetentionJob` (new)
- **Queue**: `:default`
- **Schedule**: daily
- **Args**: `%{}`
- **What it does**: deletes `op.feedback_submissions` where `created_at < now() - 180 days`, regardless of status or authorship. The aggregate counts that matter operationally (volume by category over time) are in the warehouse (§11) with the body excluded, so the sweep costs no insight.
- **On failure**: standard Oban retry; the delete is idempotent.
- ⚠️ **This job is the only erasure guarantee anonymous submissions have.** A scheduled job as the sole guarantee for a retention obligation is the failure mode `Accounts.register/1` already calls out for abandoned signups — and with the platform scaling to zero, a cron entry may not fire. Mitigate the same way: also run the sweep opportunistically from `FeedbackController.create/2` (bounded, e.g. delete at most 100 expired rows per submission), so the obligation does not depend on a scheduler alone.

## 8. External Service Calls

### Resend (email delivery, production only)
- **Client module**: `Stacks.Workers.EmailDeliveryJob`
- **Payload**: category + admin URL. **No message body, no reader email address.**
- **Fallback**: logged locally in dev/test.
- **Circuit breaker**: the existing email path's behaviour is unchanged. A Resend outage loses a nudge, not a report.

No other external call. ⚠️ **No third-party feedback widget** — not Canny, not Intercom, not a hosted form. A widget would mean the reader's words going to a vendor before they reach the platform, an external script the CSP forbids anyway, and a data-processing relationship to disclose. On a platform whose About page promises "privacy isn't a setting here, it's the default", an embedded SaaS feedback widget would be the single most contradictory thing on the site.

## 9. Storage (R2 / Local)

N/A — and this is a deliberate scope boundary. **No screenshot attachment, no file upload, no log capture.** A screenshot of a reader's own bookshelf is an image of their library and their reading, held for as long as the report lives, with its own retention and erasure obligations, its own R2 keys, and its own leak surface — a large amount of new personal data for a modest gain in bug reproducibility. If it is ever added it is its own story, with its own consent step and its own retention sweep.

## 10. Cache Interactions

- **Cache**: `RateLimiter`, new `:feedback` bucket, keyed on the trusted client IP.
- **No other cache.** The admin queue is read live; a cached list of unread feedback would be a cached copy of personal data with no invalidation story and no erasure path.

## 11. dbt Model Dependencies

- **Model**: `stg_feedback_submissions` — proto-generated, with a **column allowlist**: `id`, `category`, `route_pattern`, `platform`, `app_version`, `status`, `triaged_at`, `created_at`, and a boolean `is_authenticated` derived from `user_id IS NOT NULL`.
  ⛔ **`message`, `contact_email`, `user_id`, and `triaged_by_id` must be excluded.** The `wh` schema has no erasure path, so a free-text column copied there is permanently beyond `delete_user_data/2`'s reach — the single worst thing this story could do. `user_id` is excluded too: with it, an analyst can rejoin the warehouse copy to a person, which defeats the point of excluding the body. Assert the exclusion in the dbt schema test, and add a `stg_` column assertion so a later `mix proto.sync` regeneration cannot silently widen it.
- **Model**: `mart_feedback_volume` — daily counts by category and `is_authenticated`. Answers the only questions worth asking in aggregate: is feedback arriving, is it mostly bugs or mostly confusion, and did a release change either.
- **Trigger**: `feedback.submitted` via `DbtRefreshHandler`.
- **Consumer**: admin platform stats. ⚠️ The "Something's confusing" count is also the success metric for the FAQ (US-15.4.1).

## 12. Elm Frontend State Machine (Detail)

### Route
- **Route variant**: `Route.Feedback` — `/feedback`, the full-page framing and the link target for anything outside the app.
- **Public or authenticated**: public
- The overlay framing needs no route. It is shell state, like the connectivity banner, so it can open over any page without changing the URL — a reader reporting a bug must not lose the page the bug is on.

### Init
- **`initPage` branch**: `Route.Feedback → ( PageFeedback (FeedbackModal.init { presentation = Page, route = Nothing, isSignedIn = … }), focusCmd )`
- **Overlay init**: `Main`'s `OpenedFeedback` sets `feedback = Just (FeedbackModal.init { presentation = Overlay, route = Just currentRoute, isSignedIn = … })`
- **API calls on init**: none
- **Initial model state**: `category = Nothing`, `message = ""`, `wantsReply = False`, `contactEmail = ""`, `submitState = NotAsked`

### Model
```
{ presentation : Presentation      -- Overlay | Page
, category : Maybe Category
, message : String
, wantsReply : Bool
, contactEmail : String
, contactEmailValidation : FieldValidation
, submitState : RemoteData Http.Error ()
, confirmingDiscard : Bool
, routePattern : Maybe String
}
```

### ⚠️ `Route.toPattern` — the reason the captured context is safe
Add to `frontend/src/Navigation/Route.elm`:
```
toPattern : Route -> String
toPattern route =
    case route of
        Profile _ -> "/u/:handle"
        BlogPost _ -> "/blog/:id"
        Bookshelf name -> "/bookshelf/:name"
        …
```
`Route.toPath` would give `/u/mara` — **another reader's handle**, captured into a support record without their involvement, on a page about respecting people's data. `toPattern` gives `/u/:handle`, which is all a bug report needs. This function is the whole reason the "never anyone else's name or handle" line in the disclosure is true, and it is the one piece of this story most likely to be "simplified" back to `toPath` by someone who has not read this paragraph. Guard it with a test that asserts `toPattern` returns no route argument for every variant.

### Update cycle

| Msg | Model change | Cmd | OutMsg |
|-----|-------------|-----|--------|
| `CategoryPicked c` | `category = Just c` | None | `NoOut` |
| `MessageChanged s` | `message = s` | None | `NoOut` |
| `ToggledWantsReply` | `wantsReply` toggled | `Dom.focus "feedback-contact-email"` when revealing | `NoOut` |
| `ContactEmailChanged s` | `contactEmail = s`, revalidate | None | `NoOut` |
| `Submitted` | `submitState = Loading` | `Api.submitFeedback payload` | `NoOut` |
| `GotSubmitResponse (Ok ())` | `submitState = Success ()` | `Process.sleep 4000` then `AutoDismissed` | `NoOut` |
| `GotSubmitResponse (Err e)` | `submitState = Failure e` — **message untouched** | None | `NoOut` |
| `ClickedClose` (message non-empty) | `confirmingDiscard = True` | None | `NoOut` |
| `ClickedClose` (message empty) | — | None | `CloseRequested` |
| `ConfirmedDiscard` | — | None | `CloseRequested` |
| `AutoDismissed` | — | None | `CloseRequested` |

### `isSubmitDisabled`
Disabled when any of: `category == Nothing`; `String.length message < 10`; `String.length message > 5000`; `submitState == Loading`; `wantsReply && not isSignedIn && contactEmailValidation /= Valid`.

### View
- **Key elements**: `viewCategoryChoice` (a `fieldset` with a `legend`, four real radios), `viewMessageField`, `viewDisclosure` (expanded, `feedback-modal__disclosure`), `viewReplyToggle`, `viewActions`, `viewSuccess`, `viewDiscardConfirm`.
- **Overlay behaviour**: `role="dialog"`, `aria-modal="true"`, `aria-labelledby` on the heading. Focus trapped inside; `Escape` maps to `ClickedClose`; focus returns to the opening control on close. The page behind gets `aria-hidden="true"`.
- ⚠️ **The affordance is a link in the footer and an item in the user menu — not a floating widget.** A fixed corner button sits over the bookcase forever to say nothing most of the time, and covers a control the reader may be reaching for. This is the same reasoning already written into the shell's connectivity banner: it renders above the nav and pushes the page down rather than overlaying it, and nothing renders when there is nothing to say.
- **ARIA on submission**: the outcome is announced through an `aria-live="polite"` region, so the acknowledgement is not a visual-only event.
- **CSS classes**: `feedback-modal`, `feedback-modal__overlay`, `feedback-modal__card`, `feedback-modal__lede`, `feedback-modal__categories`, `feedback-modal__category`, `feedback-modal__message`, `feedback-modal__count`, `feedback-modal__count--warning`, `feedback-modal__disclosure`, `feedback-modal__disclosure-list`, `feedback-modal__reply-toggle`, `feedback-modal__actions`, `feedback-modal__success`, `feedback-modal__discard-confirm`, `app-footer__feedback`, `page--feedback`.
  ⚠️ **All sixteen need rules in `frontend/css/main.css` in the same change.** Follow the existing modal precedents (`Components.BlockUserModal`, `Components.RemoveBookModal`) and reuse their overlay/card rules rather than adding a third set. ## 13. Operational Metrics

- **Submission counter**: `:telemetry.execute([:stacks, :feedback, :submitted], %{count: 1}, %{category: …, authenticated: …})`. Both labels are bounded and derived from validated values, never from raw input, so `stacks_feedback_submitted_count_total{category=…,authenticated=…}` stays low-cardinality.
- **Rejection counter**: `%{result: :too_short | :too_long | :invalid_category | :invalid_route_pattern | :invalid_email}`. A spike in `invalid_route_pattern` means the client is sending concrete paths — the `toPattern` regression, caught by a metric rather than by an audit.
- **Rate-limit rejections**: `stacks_rate_limit_rejected_count_total{bucket="feedback"}`. A sustained rise from a small number of IPs is spam; a broad rise means the limit is too tight for real readers.
- **Triage queue depth**: gauge of `status = 'new'`. The number the owner actually needs; it is also the honest measure of whether the channel is being *served* rather than just being open.
- **Time-to-triage**: histogram of `triaged_at - created_at`. A closed beta earns its trust here — feedback that is never acknowledged teaches readers not to send any.
- **Notification email outcomes**: `EmailDeliveryJob` completed/failed for template `feedback_received`, plus a counter for coalesced (skipped) notifications so the 15-minute suppression is visible rather than looking like loss.
- **Retention sweep**: rows deleted per `FeedbackRetentionJob` run, and rows deleted by the opportunistic path in `create/2`. ⚠️ If the scheduled job's count is always zero while the opportunistic count is not, the scheduler is not firing — the scale-to-zero failure mode, visible only because both are counted.
- **Zero-row sweep** (verification, not monitoring): after driving the flow live, assert `op.feedback_submissions` is non-empty, `oban_jobs` contains a `feedback_received` job whose args carry **no** `message` key, and `wh.stg_feedback_submissions` has no `message` column. A zero row count in the first is a wiring break, not a fixture gap.

## 14. Performance & Usability Metrics

- **Submit latency**: one insert, one event, one job enqueue. Target p95 < 100ms. If it is slower, the opportunistic retention delete in `create/2` is doing too much per call — lower its bound.
- **Panel open to send**: median time from `OpenedFeedback` to `Submitted`. A very short median suggests low-effort submissions; a very long one suggests the textarea is intimidating.
- **Abandonment rate**: panels opened and closed without sending. High abandonment with non-empty drafts (visible via the discard confirm) means something in the form is putting people off — most likely the disclosure block, which should then be tested shorter rather than removed.
- **Disclosure engagement**: not instrumented, deliberately. Measuring whether readers read the privacy disclosure would require watching them read it, which is the thing the disclosure promises we do not do.
- **Category distribution**: the shape of a beta. Heavy on "Something's confusing" points at the FAQ and the onboarding flow; heavy on "Something's broken" points at the verification pass.
- **Signed-out share**: submissions with `authenticated: false`. If it is non-trivial, allowing anonymous submission was the right call and the login-page affordance is earning its place.
- **Draft-preservation check**: an E2E assertion rather than a metric — fail the request, assert the textarea still contains the text. ⚠️ Drive this with a real intercepted request; and patch **XHR**, not `fetch`, to observe `elm/http`.
- **Repeat submitters**: readers sending more than three reports. These are the beta's most valuable people and the ones most likely to hit the rate limit — check the limit against their actual behaviour before defending it.

## 15. Cost Tracking

- **Resend**: one email per submission at ~$0.001, with 15-minute coalescing capping a burst. At beta volume — tens of submissions a month — this is cents.
- **Neon compute**: one small insert per submission; one indexed query per admin page view; one bounded delete per submission plus a daily sweep. Negligible.
- **Fly.io compute**: an insert, an event emit, and a job enqueue. Immaterial.
- **Storage growth**: `op.feedback_submissions` rows are a few KB at most and are capped at 180 days. Unbounded growth is impossible by construction.
- **No third-party feedback tool**: a hosted feedback SaaS starts around $50–$100/month and would carry a data-processing relationship. Cost is the least of the reasons not to use one (§8), but it is not nothing.
- **Total per submission**: ~$0.001, dominated by the notification email.
