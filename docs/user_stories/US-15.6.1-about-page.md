# US-15.6.1 — Read What the Platform Is and Why

## 1. User Story

> **As a** stranger who has just arrived, **I want to** find out what this is and who runs it **so that** I can decide whether to trust it with anything.

**What the reader wants:** a straight answer to "what is this, who made it, and what does it
want from me" before creating an account.

**What they get:**
- What the platform is for, in the owner's voice rather than marketing register.
- What it refuses to do — no ads, no selling reading habits, no algorithmic feed.
- The licence stated accurately: **source available**, not open source, with the reason.
- Onward links to the cost page and the metrics surface, so the claims are checkable rather than
  asserted.

**Acceptance criteria:**
- Reads as finished prose to a signed-out visitor.
- Licence wording matches `LICENSE` and every other claim site — one register everywhere.
- The onward links resolve.

## 2. UI Interaction Flow

### Happy path
1. The reader opens `/about` — directly, or from a link on another public page.
2. The page renders.

### Sad paths
- **Content fails to load** (where the page fetches): a failure state that reads as a failure, not
  as an empty or zero-valued page.

## 3. API Calls

None — the SPA renders this route from static content.

## 4. Auth & Middleware Guards

Public. No authentication, no visibility resolution, no age gate. This page is written to be read
by someone who has not signed up and may never — gating it would defeat its purpose.

## 5–11. Database, Events, Jobs, External Services, Storage, Cache, dbt

**None.** A page that reads from nothing cannot show a reader something stale.

## 12. Elm Frontend State Machine (Detail)

- **Route**: `Route.About` — `/about`, public pipeline.
- **Init**: no fetch — the content is static prose in the module.
- **View**: `Page.About`; headings, lede, and the licence paragraph.

## Why this story exists

Written 2026-08-20. This route shipped and was **live to the public with no user story** — one of
four such routes found by the campaign's story census. A public page with no story is a page whose
purpose is recorded nowhere: nothing states who it is for or what it must not do, so nothing can
notice when it drifts from either.
